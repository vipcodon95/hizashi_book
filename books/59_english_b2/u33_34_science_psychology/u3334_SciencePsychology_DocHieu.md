## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 33) — Báo cáo dự án hội chợ khoa học của Tina

> **Science Fair Report: Do Native Flowers Attract More Pollinators?**
>
> Written by: Tina Carter, Grade 7
>
> Question
>
> A pollinator is defined as any animal that carries pollen from one flower to another. Pollinators can be divided into several groups, including bees, butterflies, flies, beetles and some birds. My question was: in this garden, do native flowers, which grow naturally in Southern California, attract more pollinators than flowers from other parts of the world?
>
> Method
>
> I did my research in the garden behind the gym at Westview High, where my brother goes to school. It was planted two years ago by the Green Club, and its first native flowers are said to have been grown from seeds that the science teacher collected himself. I chose four native plants and four non-native plants of similar size and color. On twelve sunny mornings in March, I watched each plant for ten minutes and counted every visit by a pollinator. To make the test fair, I changed the order in which I watched the plants every day, so that no plant was always watched at the warmest time.
>
> Results
>
> I counted 412 visits to the native plants and 168 visits to the non-native plants, which is almost two and a half times as many. Native bees, most of which were very small, made up the largest group of visitors. On two windy mornings, the counts were much lower for all plants, so those results seem to have been affected by the weather.
>
> Conclusion
>
> In this garden, native flowers attracted many more pollinators. However, I only studied eight plants in one garden, so more research is needed before anyone can say that this is true everywhere. A personal note: on the day of the fair, I was sure that everyone could hear my voice shaking. Afterward, one of the judges told me that I had seemed "very calm". I wish I had known that before I started.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 34) — Hiệu ứng ánh đèn sân khấu

> **Nobody Is Watching as Closely as You Think**
>
> Have you ever walked into a room with a coffee stain on your shirt and spent the whole meeting wishing that you were invisible? If so, you have experienced what psychologists call the spotlight effect: the feeling that other people are noticing us far more than they really are.
>
> In a well-known experiment in 2000, researchers at an American university asked students to put on a T-shirt with a large picture of a singer who was considered deeply uncool at the time. Each student then walked into a room where other students were sitting, stayed for a short time and left. Afterward, the students who had worn the shirt were asked to guess how many people in the room had noticed it. On average, they guessed about half. In fact, only about a quarter had noticed it.
>
> Why do we overestimate how much others notice us? One reason is simple: we are the center of our own world. We know exactly what we look like, what we said and how nervous we feel, so we assume that others can see it too. But other people are busy with their own worries. They are far more likely to be thinking about their own coffee stains than about yours.
>
> A related idea is the "illusion of transparency": the belief that our feelings are easy for others to see. People giving a speech often wish that the audience couldn't see how nervous they are, but studies have found that listeners notice much less nervousness than speakers expect. In one study, simply telling speakers about this illusion before they spoke helped them to feel calmer and give better speeches.
>
> So the next time you wish that you could take back an embarrassing moment, remember that most people have probably forgotten it already, if they noticed it at all. The spotlight that you feel is mostly a light that you are shining on yourself.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Báo cáo khoa học có mục rõ ràng (`Question`, `Method`, `Results`, `Conclusion`). Dò đúng mục để tìm câu trả lời.
- `is believed to have been + V3` / `seem to have been + V3`: nói về việc trong quá khứ mà người viết không chắc chắn hoàn toàn.
- Bài thí nghiệm tâm lý: phân biệt con số người tham gia ĐOÁN (`about half`) với con số THỰC TẾ (`about a quarter`).
- Câu "more research is needed" thể hiện thái độ thận trọng. Đừng chọn phương án "sure that it is true everywhere".
