## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 29) — Bản tin: bà Harriet suýt bị lừa qua điện thoại

> **"Your Son Is in Trouble": How a Phone Scam Almost Worked**
>
> Maple Street Neighborhood News · by Corinne Whitfield
>
> Last Tuesday afternoon, Harriet Brooks was closing her café when her phone rang. A man who said he was a police officer told her that her son, Nathan, had been arrested after a car accident and needed $3,000 immediately. The money, he said, had to be paid in gift cards from a nearby store, and she must not tell anyone, because the case was "confidential".
>
> "I was shaking," Harriet told me. "He knew Nathan's name and the color of his car. For a moment, I believed every word." But something felt wrong. "No police officer has ever asked me to pay with gift cards," she said. "And why couldn't I hear Nathan's voice?" She told the caller that she needed to find her glasses, hung up, and called her daughter-in-law, Helena, who was sitting next to Nathan at home, watching a movie.
>
> Officer Grant of the San Diego Police Department says that this kind of scam is reported every week. "Scammers can find a surprising amount of information online, such as names, family photos and even cars," she explains. "That's what makes the story believable." "Real police will never ask for payment by gift card, and they will never tell you to keep a call secret from your family. If someone does, it's a scam."
>
> Not everyone is as lucky as Harriet. Officer Grant says that thousands of dollars could have been saved in some recent cases if the calls had been checked with a family member first. Calls like these should be reported to the police, even when no money is lost, because each report helps officers track down the people behind them. Harriet has since agreed on a family "code word" with Nathan and Helena, a word that only they know and that can be used to check whether a call is real. She refused to tell me what it is. "Nice try, Corinne," she said.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 30) — Cách bất đồng mà không mất lòng nhau

> **How to Disagree Without Falling Out**
>
> Every family has a topic that turns a pleasant dinner into a battle of raised voices, whether it is screen time, money, or whether pineapple belongs on pizza. Rarely does anyone change their mind during these arguments, and often people leave the table feeling worse about each other than before.
>
> Psychologists who study conflict say that the problem is usually not the disagreement itself, but how it happens. When we feel attacked, our bodies react as if we were in danger: our hearts beat faster, and we stop listening and start defending ourselves. No sooner has one person raised their voice than the other raises theirs, and soon nobody is talking about the original question at all.
>
> So what helps? First, ask before you argue. A question such as "What makes you see it that way?" does more than any clever point, because people are far more willing to listen once they feel that they have been heard. Second, look for what you share. Two parents who disagree about phones may both want the same thing: a child who is happy and safe. Starting from that shared goal changes the whole conversation. Third, talk about yourself, not the other person: "I worry when…" is easier to hear than "You always…".
>
> Not only does this approach keep the peace, but it also makes it more likely that someone will actually change their mind, perhaps not at the table, but days later, when nobody is watching. And sometimes, the best result is simply to agree to disagree.
>
> Finally, know when to stop. If voices are rising, it is perfectly fine to say, "Let's take a break and come back to this." Never has an argument been improved by continuing it at the top of your voice. A family can survive many disagreements. It is much harder to survive the habit of not listening.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Bị động với modal: `should be + V3` (nên được), `could have been + V3` (lẽ ra đã có thể được, nhưng không). Câu hỏi nghĩa hay hỏi điều thực tế đã xảy ra.
- Đảo ngữ phủ định (`Rarely does…`, `Never has…`, `No sooner…than`) nhấn mạnh. Viết lại theo trật tự thường để hiểu nghĩa.
- Bản tin lừa đảo: lời khuyên của cảnh sát thường nằm trong ngoặc kép, có `never`. Câu hỏi "What does the officer say…" lấy đúng chỗ này.
- Bài tư vấn đánh số (`First`, `Second`, `Third`, `Finally`): mỗi lời khuyên đi kèm một ví dụ. Câu hỏi chức năng hỏi ví dụ minh hoạ cho lời khuyên nào.
