## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 31) — Thành phố suýt cạn nước: "Ngày số 0" ở Cape Town

> **Day Zero: The City That Almost Ran Out of Water**
>
> In early 2018, the four million people of Cape Town, South Africa, were told something that seemed impossible for a modern city: their taps might soon run dry. After three years of unusually low rainfall, the dams that supplied the city were less than a third full. City officials announced a date, called "Day Zero", when most taps would be turned off and residents would have to collect water from around two hundred collection points.
>
> For the plan to work, everyone had to change their habits, and quickly. Residents were asked to use no more than fifty liters each per day, about the amount that a typical American shower uses in six or seven minutes. People collected the water from their showers to flush toilets, washed less often, and stopped watering their gardens. Some restaurants served food on paper plates to avoid washing dishes. It was not easy for families with small children, or for people who lived in crowded areas with shared taps, to follow the rules.
>
> The result was remarkable. Within a few months, the city's daily water use had fallen by around half. Day Zero was pushed back again and again, and when the winter rains finally arrived in the middle of 2018, it was cancelled. The taps never ran dry.
>
> Cape Town is not the only city at risk. Experts warn that, as the climate changes and cities grow, droughts of this kind are likely to become more common, from southern Europe to parts of the United States. It is important for cities to plan ahead: to repair leaking pipes, to collect rainwater and to reuse water that has already been cleaned. But the most surprising lesson from Cape Town was about people. When it was clear what was at stake, millions of ordinary residents changed the way they lived in just a few months.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 32) — Lịch sử khách sạn Hotel del Coronado

> **The Hotel by the Sea: A Short History of the Hotel del Coronado**
>
> Across the bay from downtown San Diego stands one of the most photographed buildings in California: a huge white hotel with red roofs and towers, sitting right on the beach. The Hotel del Coronado, known to locals simply as "the Del", opened in 1888, when the area around it was little more than sand and bushes.
>
> The hotel was the idea of two businessmen from the Midwest, who had bought the land in the 1880s and dreamed of building a resort that people would talk about all over the country. Because there were not enough skilled builders in the area, workers were brought from other cities, and a factory was set up nearby to produce the wood and bricks. Remarkably, the main building was finished in about eleven months.
>
> At the time, it was the largest resort hotel in the world, and it is still one of the largest wooden buildings in the United States. Its owners boasted about its modern comforts: guests were offered electric lights, telephones and even an elevator, which many visitors had never seen before. Over the following decades, the hotel was visited by writers, film stars and wealthy families from across the country, and in 1958, part of a famous Hollywood comedy was filmed there.
>
> Old wooden buildings are expensive to keep, and in the 1960s, there were serious worries that the Del might be torn down. Instead, it was bought by new owners who invested in repairs, and in 1977, the hotel was given the status of a National Historic Landmark, one of the highest honors that a building can receive in the United States.
>
> Today, the Del is still a working hotel, and its long wooden corridors are still walked by thousands of guests every week. Visitors who are not staying there are welcome to walk through the main lobby and along the beach. Standing there, it is easy to understand why its owners believed, over a hundred and thirty years ago, that people would one day travel across the country just to see it.

**20. Use both texts. What do the stories of Cape Town and the Del have in common?**
*(Dựa vào cả hai bài. Câu chuyện Cape Town và the Del có điểm gì chung?)*

- A. Both of them are about hotels. *(Cả hai đều về khách sạn.)*
- B. Something valuable was saved because people chose to act before it was too late. *(Một thứ quý giá đã được cứu vì con người chọn hành động trước khi quá muộn.)*
- C. Both of them happened in 1888. *(Cả hai đều xảy ra năm 1888.)*
- D. Both of them were caused by rain. *(Cả hai đều do mưa gây ra.)*

---

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `For + N + to V` và `It is + adj + for + N + to V`: phần sau for là ai/cái gì thực hiện hành động.
- Bị động với người nhận làm chủ ngữ (`guests were offered…`, `the hotel was given…`): hỏi "ai nhận được gì" là ra đáp án.
- Bài lịch sử: ghi các mốc (1888, thập niên 1960, 1977). Câu hỏi hay tráo sự kiện giữa các mốc.
- Đoạn "It was not easy for…" là đoạn cân bằng: người viết không chỉ khen. Câu hỏi mục đích hay hỏi đoạn này.
