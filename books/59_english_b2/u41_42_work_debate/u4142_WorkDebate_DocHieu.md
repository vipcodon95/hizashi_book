# Units 41–42 — The future of work & Debate and argument (Tương lai việc làm & Tranh biện)

> **Luyện cho:** Unit 41 + Unit 42 khoá B2 · phiếu `b2.161` – `b2.168` (although / whereas và despite / in spite of, danh từ hoá, tương lai việc làm; giới từ + từ để hỏi, How + tính từ!, câu không hoàn chỉnh, tranh biện).
>
> **Nhân vật:** Nathan Brooks, Lydia Park · tháng 8, năm 7
>
> **Mục tiêu:** đọc một email công việc và một buổi tranh biện; hiểu `although / whereas` (+ mệnh đề) với `despite / in spite of` (+ danh từ), danh từ hoá trong văn phong công việc (a reduction in…, the introduction of…), câu hỏi giới từ + wh- (On what evidence? For whom…?), câu cảm thán `How interesting!`, câu không hoàn chỉnh khi chốt ý, và câu hỏi tu từ.

## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 41) — Bức thư đầu tiên của Nathan gửi nhóm khi lên trưởng nhóm

> **From Your New Team Lead: How We Will Work Together**
>
> Hi everyone,
>
> As most of you know, I am now leading our team of nine software engineers. I have worked alongside many of you for years, so this is a strange change for me too. Before I make any changes, I want to explain how I would like us to work, and why.
>
> Our biggest challenge is distance. Three of us work in San Diego, four are in Chicago and two are in Lisbon, which is eight hours ahead of California. Although video calls are useful, they are also tiring, and the number of meetings has grown every year. Last month, I counted that we each spent an average of eleven hours a week in calls.
>
> So here is my proposal. First, a reduction in meetings. We will keep one team call a week, at a time that is reasonable in all three cities, and every other update will be written. Second, the introduction of "core hours": two hours a day, from 8 to 10 a.m. in San Diego, when everyone agrees to be online and available. Outside those hours, nobody should expect an immediate reply, whereas urgent problems can always be sent to me by phone. Third, every project will have one clear owner. In spite of our good intentions, too many decisions have been delayed because everyone thought that someone else was responsible.
>
> I know that some of you prefer talking to writing, and that written updates can feel cold. Despite that, I think that clear writing will give us back time that we can use for actual work, and, I hope, for our lives. We will try this for two months and then decide together whether to keep it.
>
> One last thing: I am new at this. If something isn't working, please tell me directly. I would much rather hear it from you than discover it in six months.
>
> Thanks, Nathan

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 42) — Tranh biện: có cần bằng đại học để có việc tốt?

> **Debate Club: Do You Need a College Degree to Get a Good Job?**
>
> Ms. Park's debate club is now in its second year. This week, Maddie and Noah, who started on opposite sides at their very first meeting, faced each other again.
>
> **Lydia:** Maddie, you are arguing that a degree is no longer necessary. On what evidence?
>
> **Maddie:** Three pieces. First, only about two in five American adults have a four-year degree, so most workers are succeeding without one. Second, a growing number of employers, including some very large companies, have removed degree requirements from many jobs and now hire based on skills. Third, cost. Many students finish college with years of debt. For whom is that a good deal, if a shorter training program leads to the same job?
>
> **Lydia:** Thank you. Noah?
>
> **Noah:** I agree that a degree isn't the only path. Electricians, nurses with two-year qualifications, many tech workers: real careers, no four-year degree. But on average, people with a bachelor's degree still earn considerably more over their lifetimes, and they are less likely to be unemployed. And removing a requirement on paper isn't the same as hiring people without degrees. Studies have found that, at many companies, the change has made very little difference to who actually gets hired. So, a degree: not necessary for everyone, but still very useful for many.
>
> **Lydia:** How interesting! You both used the word "many" in your final sentence.
>
> **Maddie:** That's because neither of us thinks there's one answer for everyone.
>
> **Noah:** Exactly. To whom you're talking matters too. A future nurse and a future electrician need very different advice.
>
> Ms. Park smiled. A year ago, they had shouted at each other about phones. Today, they had disagreed more politely, and more usefully, than many adults she knew.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `although / whereas + mệnh đề`, `despite / in spite of + danh từ / V-ing`. Câu hỏi ngữ pháp và nghĩa hay hỏi phần sau các từ này.
- Danh từ hoá (`a reduction in meetings`) làm câu gọn, trang trọng. Đổi lại thành động từ (reduce meetings) để hiểu nhanh.
- Câu hỏi tu từ (`For whom is that a good deal?`): người nói không cần câu trả lời, mà ngầm nói điều ngược lại.
- Tranh biện: mỗi người có `First / Second / Third` và một câu chốt. Câu hỏi so sánh hai phía lấy từ các câu chốt này.
