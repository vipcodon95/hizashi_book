## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 13) — Quán Harriet's ra mắt "Bàn ăn của hàng xóm"

> **This Fall at Harriet's: The Neighbors' Table**
>
> Dear friends, for thirteen years, you have eaten my soups and my pies. This fall, it's my turn to taste yours. Every Friday from September to November, one special dish will be served at lunch, and every one of them will come from a family on Maple Street. The cooks will be in the kitchen with me, and the story behind each recipe will be written on the board by the door.
>
> Here is the first part of our schedule:
>
> September 4: Jollof rice from the Okonkwo family
>
> Grandma Okonkwo warns that every family from her hometown argues about the right amount of pepper, and that hers is "the correct one". You have been warned.
>
> September 11: Chicken tamales from Mr. Alvarez
>
> His mother made them every winter, and he says that you have to chop the chicken by hand, not in a machine. We will be wrapping them by hand all morning.
>
> September 18: Peach pie from my own mother's recipe
>
> I have changed only one thing: less sugar. If she knew, she would not be pleased.
>
> September 25: Banana bread from Tina Carter
>
> Our youngest cook found the recipe in her great-grandmother's notebook, written in pencil on a page covered in old flour. She has tested it four times, and her family says that they never want to see a banana again.
>
> Would you like to share a dish? Please talk to me at the counter. We will ask for a list of ingredients first. If so, we will plan a test day together; if not, don't worry, there are still plenty of Fridays. If necessary, we will change a recipe slightly so that people with allergies can enjoy it too, but only with your permission.
>
> Two small promises. First, the price will stay the same as our usual lunch, because this is a celebration, not a business plan. Second, nothing will be thrown away. Any food that is not eaten up by two o'clock will be taken to the Harbor Street Shelter.
>
> Food is the way that many of us remember where we come from. Let's taste each other's memories.
>
> Harriet

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 14) — Du lịch quá tải và cách các điểm đến xoay xở

> **When Too Many Visitors Love a Place**
>
> Having saved for months, millions of travelers arrive every summer at the same famous places, all hoping for the same photo. The result has a name: overtourism, which happens when a destination receives more visitors than it can handle.
>
> Venice is perhaps the best-known example. Fewer than 50,000 people now live in its historic center, while millions of visitors walk through its narrow streets every year. In 2024, the city began charging day visitors a small entry fee on its busiest days, the first city in the world to do so. In Kyoto, Japan, some private lanes in the old Gion district were closed to tourists after residents complained that visitors were following people and taking photos without asking. Cruise ships can make the problem worse: a single large ship can bring several thousand passengers into a small town for just a few hours, and many of them spend very little money there before they return to the ship for lunch.
>
> Nature suffers too. Maya Bay in Thailand, made famous by a Hollywood film, was receiving several thousand visitors a day before it was closed in 2018. Having been left alone for more than three years, its coral and sea life began to recover, and when the bay reopened, the number of visitors was strictly limited and swimming was banned.
>
> Not everyone agrees with these limits. Many local businesses depend on visitors, and some people argue that fees simply make travel a privilege for the wealthy. Others point out that a place which is destroyed by its own popularity will soon have no visitors at all.
>
> Travelers can help, too. Visiting outside the busiest months, staying longer in one place rather than rushing between five, and choosing places that few people have heard of all reduce the pressure. Eating in small restaurants run by local families and buying from local shops also means that more of your money stays in the community. Above all, remember that the streets you are photographing are someone's home.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `will be + V3` = sẽ được làm. Thông báo của quán, sự kiện hay dùng cấu trúc này.
- `If so` / `If not` thay cho cả một mệnh đề vừa nói. Đọc câu ngay trước để biết "so" là gì.
- `Having + V3, …` = sau khi đã… `Having been + V3` = sau khi đã được / bị…
- Lời nhân vật có thể khoanh vùng phong tục (`every family from her hometown argues…`). Đừng chọn phương án gán cách làm cho cả một vùng.
