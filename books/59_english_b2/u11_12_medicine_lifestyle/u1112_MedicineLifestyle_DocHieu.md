## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 11) — Thuốc mới được thử nghiệm thế nào

> **From the Lab to the Pharmacy: How New Medicines Are Tested**
>
> Right now, thousands of possible new medicines are being tested around the world. Most of them will never reach a pharmacy. Of the drugs that are tested on people for the first time, only about one in ten is finally approved, and the whole process usually takes between ten and fifteen years.
>
> Before a medicine is given to any person, it has usually been studied for years in laboratories, first on cells and then on animals. Only then do clinical trials begin, and they are divided into stages, or "phases". In Phase 1, a small group of twenty to a hundred volunteers, often healthy adults, take the drug so that researchers can check whether it is safe and what dose the body can handle. In Phase 2, several hundred patients who have the disease are given the drug, and the question becomes whether it actually works. Phase 3 is the biggest: hundreds or thousands of patients are divided into groups, and the new drug is compared with an existing treatment or with a placebo, a pill that contains no medicine. In many trials, neither the patients nor the doctors know who is receiving which, so that their expectations cannot affect the results.
>
> Admittedly, this system is slow and expensive, and patients who are seriously ill sometimes cannot wait. While it is true that some rules have been relaxed in emergencies, most scientists argue that the stages are necessary. Nevertheless, they continue to look for ways to make trials faster without making them less safe.
>
> Every volunteer must also give consent: they must be told about the possible risks and be free to leave the trial at any time. "Patients often ask me if they should join a trial," says Dr. Leah Carter, an emergency doctor at Sunset Bay Hospital. "I tell them to ask two questions: what are the risks, and what happens if I want to stop? If those two questions aren't answered clearly, the former matters more than any promise." The process may be slow, but every medicine in your cabinet at home has passed through it.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 12) — Ba mươi buổi sáng chậm của Mitchell và Megan

> **Thirty Slow Mornings**
>
> Written by: Mitchell Carter, for the Westview High staff newsletter
>
> For years, my mornings looked the same. The alarm went off at 6:15, I checked my email in bed, drank coffee standing up and left the house at 6:45, already tired. My wife Megan, an emergency nurse, had it even worse: after a night shift, she would come home at dawn just as I was about to leave. In June, we decided to try an experiment: thirty "slow mornings".
>
> The rules were simple. We would get up thirty minutes earlier, leave our phones in the kitchen overnight, eat breakfast sitting down, and spend ten minutes outside before work. On the days after Megan's night shifts, we agreed that she would sleep and I would do the routine alone.
>
> The first week was harder than either of us had expected. Getting up earlier meant going to bed earlier, and we kept staying up to finish one more episode of a TV series. On day four, I dozed off on the sofa at nine in the evening, and our son Trevor kindly took a photo for the family chat.
>
> By the second week, however, something had changed. Without my phone, I noticed things: the birds in the lemon tree, the light coming slowly over the hills, the way our dog sleeps with one eye open. Megan and I talked over breakfast more in a month than we had in the previous year. I arrived at school calmer, and my first-period students noticed; one of them asked me, suspiciously, whether I was feeling well.
>
> Not everything worked. Our teenagers refused to join, and on busy days the ten minutes outside were sometimes skipped. But we have kept the most important rule: the phones sleep in the kitchen. It is a small change, and I am not claiming that it will work for everyone. For us, it has turned the start of the day from a race into a conversation.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Bài giải thích quy trình: đánh dấu từng bước (`Phase 1`, `Phase 2`…) và mục đích của mỗi bước. Câu hỏi hay tráo mục đích giữa các bước.
- Đoạn mở bằng `Admittedly` / `While it is true that` thường là nhượng bộ; ý chính của người viết nằm sau `Nevertheless` / `However`.
- `the former` = cái nhắc trước, `the latter` = cái nhắc sau. Quay lại câu trước để đếm thứ tự.
- Giọng mỉa vui (`Trevor kindly took a photo`): nghĩa thật ngược với nghĩa chữ. Hãy hỏi việc đó có thật sự "tử tế" không.
