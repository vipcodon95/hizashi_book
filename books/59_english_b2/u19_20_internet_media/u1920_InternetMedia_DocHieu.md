## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 19) — Khi đoạn clip của Trevor nổi khắp mạng

> **Two Million Views and a Bad Ankle**
>
> It happened in the second half of a game that Westview High was losing 1–0. A player on the other team twisted his ankle and fell near the corner of the field, and Trevor, who was running past with the ball, stopped, kicked it out of play and knelt down beside him. He stayed there, talking to the boy and keeping his leg still, until the coaches arrived.
>
> Jake filmed the whole thing on his phone from the bench and posted twenty seconds of it that evening with the caption "This is my best friend." By the next morning, the clip had been shared thousands of times. By the weekend, it had two million views.
>
> At first, Trevor enjoyed it. Strangers called him a hero, and a famous player he had idolized since he was eight left a comment with a heart. But being recognized in the supermarket felt strange, and being talked about by people he had never met felt even stranger. Then the unkind comments began. Some people claimed that the whole thing had been planned for the camera. Others mocked the other team, and a few anonymous accounts were harsh about the injured boy.
>
> "I hated that," Trevor said later. "He was the one who got hurt, and people were making fun of him because of me." A local TV station asked him for an interview. After talking it over with his parents, he agreed, but only on one condition: the boy, whose name was Diego, had to be invited too. In the interview, Trevor said that any player would have done the same, and Diego said that Trevor had told him a terrible joke to stop him from crying.
>
> Within a week, the clip had stopped being shared, and the internet had moved on to a dog that could skateboard. Trevor was relieved. "It was nice for a few days," he admitted. "But I'd rather be known by the people who actually know me." He and Diego still message each other. They have agreed to meet when their teams play again in the spring.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 20) — Ai thật sự chọn thứ bạn xem?

> **Who Really Chooses What You Watch?**
>
> You open an app to watch one short video, and forty minutes later, you are still there. It was not you who chose most of those videos. It was a recommendation algorithm, a computer program that decides what to show you next.
>
> These systems are now behind much of what we watch, read and listen to. Some years ago, one of the largest video platforms said that more than 70 percent of the time that people spent watching videos on its site came from recommendations, and a major streaming service reported a similar figure for the shows that its members watched.
>
> What most people don't realize is how the algorithm learns. It notices everything: which videos you finish, which ones you skip, where you pause and what you watch late at night. Its main goal is usually to keep you watching, because the longer you stay, the more advertisements you see. Content that makes people feel strong emotions, whether delight or anger, tends to keep them watching, so that kind of content is often pushed to the top.
>
> Some critics argue that this traps people in "filter bubbles", where they only see opinions that they already agree with. The research on this is, to some extent, mixed. Arguably, many people still meet different views online, but the algorithm can make the most extreme ones seem far more common than they really are. It is also worth remembering that algorithms are not evil. They help you find a song that you love or a cooking video that solves your problem.
>
> So how can you stay in control? Experts suggest searching for what you want instead of only scrolling, following a few sources that you sometimes disagree with, and clearing your watch history now and then to see what changes. Above all, notice how you feel. If an app leaves you angrier or more tired than when you opened it, it is not you who is winning.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `being + V3` = bị / được (người khác) làm gì. Câu hỏi nghĩa hay đảo vai: ai làm, ai chịu.
- Câu chẻ `It was X who/that…` và `What … is…` nhấn mạnh một phần của câu. Đáp án là phần được nhấn mạnh.
- Từ rào đón (`tend to`, `arguably`, `to some extent`) cho thấy người viết thận trọng. Câu hỏi "why does the writer use…" có đáp án kiểu "avoid claiming too much".
- Câu hỏi chức năng chi tiết ("Why is it important that…?"): hỏi xem chi tiết đó làm hành động của nhân vật có ý nghĩa thế nào.
