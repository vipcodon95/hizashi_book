## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 23) — Câu lạc bộ sách bàn về Frankenstein

> **Book Club Notes: Frankenstein, Two Hundred Years Later**
>
> Written by: Lydia Park, for the book club at Harriet's
>
> Dear all, thank you for a wonderful evening. For those of you who missed it, here is what we talked about. Most of us had never read Frankenstein before, although all of us thought we knew the story. The first surprise was the author. Written by Mary Shelley when she was only eighteen, the novel began as part of a competition between friends, who were staying near a lake in Switzerland one rainy summer and challenged each other to write a ghost story. When the book first came out in 1818, her name did not appear on it.
>
> The second surprise was the monster, or rather the creature, as the book calls him. Frankenstein is not his name; it is the name of the young scientist who makes him and then runs away in horror. The creature in the novel is nothing like the slow, silent figure in old films. He teaches himself to read, he speaks beautifully, and he is desperate to be loved. Rejected by everyone he meets because of his appearance, he becomes cruel only after years of loneliness.
>
> This led to our liveliest discussion. Who is the real monster: the creature, or the man who created him and refused to take responsibility? Ethan argued that Victor Frankenstein was simply a young man who panicked. Harriet, sitting by the window with her third cup of tea, said that panicking is not an excuse when you have made a living person. Nathan pointed out that the questions in the book feel very modern: what do scientists owe to the things that they create?
>
> Not everyone loved it. Several people found the long letters at the beginning slow, and the man sitting next to me admitted that he had fallen asleep twice in the first chapter. But almost all of us agreed that a story written two hundred years ago by a teenager had something important to say to us today.
>
> Next month: something lighter, I promise. Lydia

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 24) — Vì sao thể thao nữ ngày càng nhiều người xem

> **The Rise of Women's Sports: Why Fans Are Finally Watching**
>
> In April 2024, something happened that would have seemed impossible a few years earlier. The final of the college women's basketball championship in the United States was watched by about 18.9 million people on television, more than the men's final that year. It was the first time in history that this had happened.
>
> For decades, people who ran sports television argued that women's sports were not popular enough to show. But many fans and researchers point out that the reasoning went in a circle: women's games were not shown because nobody watched them, and nobody watched them because they were not shown. Not being on television meant fewer fans, fewer sponsors and less money for the players.
>
> Several things have helped to break that circle. More games are now shown on television and online, at times when people can actually watch them. Star players have built huge followings on social media, so that fans feel that they know them personally. And many young fans, both girls and boys, simply do not share the idea that women's sports are less exciting. Attendance at women's professional basketball and soccer games has reached record levels in recent years, and many games now sell out.
>
> There is still a long way to go. Women's sports still receive a small share of sports coverage, and in most professional sports, the gap between what men and women are paid remains enormous. Some commentators are always claiming that the new interest is just a fashion that will soon pass. The numbers, so far, suggest otherwise: each new record has been followed by another.
>
> The lesson may be a simple one. People cannot become fans of something that they never see. Give them the chance, and they will decide for themselves.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Mệnh đề phân từ đầu câu (`Written by…`, `Rejected by…`) bổ nghĩa cho chủ ngữ ngay sau dấu phẩy, và thường chỉ thời gian hoặc nguyên nhân.
- Bài ghi chép thảo luận: ghi nhanh ai nói gì (Ethan, Harriet, Nathan). Câu hỏi quan điểm hay tráo ý giữa các người.
- `be always + V-ing` cho thấy người viết khó chịu với thói quen đó. Câu ngay sau thường là lời phản bác.
- Lập luận vòng tròn (A vì B, B vì A): câu hỏi "why … in a circle?" có đáp án nêu cả hai vế.
