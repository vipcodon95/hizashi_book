## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 5) — Tracy chỉ Tina cách ôn thi qua gọi video

> **A Video Call About Studying**
>
> On a Sunday evening in March, Tina called her big sister in Washington. She had a science test on Friday, and she didn't know where to begin.
>
> **Tina:** Tracy, I was wondering if you could help me. I have a test on the human body, and there are forty pages of notes. I don't even know how to start.
>
> **Tracy:** Of course. First, tell me what you usually do.
>
> **Tina:** I read my notes again and again, and I highlight the important parts in yellow. Sometimes the whole page is yellow.
>
> **Tracy:** That's what almost everyone does, and I did it too. The problem is that rereading feels like learning, because the words look familiar. But recognizing something isn't the same as remembering it. Scientists who have compared study methods found that highlighting and rereading are among the least useful ones.
>
> **Tina:** Then what am I supposed to do?
>
> **Tracy:** Test yourself. Close your notes and try to write down everything that you can remember about, say, the heart. Then open your notes and check what you missed. It's harder, and that's exactly why it works: every time you pull information out of your memory, you make it stronger.
>
> **Tina:** That sounds tiring.
>
> **Tracy:** It is, a bit. The second trick is to spread it out. Do twenty minutes tonight, twenty on Tuesday and twenty on Thursday, instead of three hours on Thursday night. Your brain needs time between sessions for things to sink in.
>
> **Tina:** And what if I get something wrong when I test myself?
>
> **Tracy:** Even better. If you get it wrong and then check the answer, you're more likely to remember it later. Mistakes are part of the process.
>
> **Tina:** Could you test me on Wednesday? Mom's working nights this week.
>
> **Tracy:** Deal. Make a list of ten questions that you think your teacher might ask, and I'll add five tricky ones.
>
> On Friday, Tina sent a photo of her test to the family chat. At the top, in red, was a large A. Below it, she had written: "Tracy, you're a genius. Don't let it go to your head."

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 6) — Email của Tracy về tuần đầu thực tập

> **Subject: First week at Brandt & Lowe!**
>
> Hi Mom and Dad,
>
> I survived my first week at Brandt & Lowe, the engineering company where I'm doing a part-time internship this semester, and I think I'm going to love it.
>
> My supervisor, Dana Okafor, a bridge engineer with twenty years of experience, is tough but fair. On my first morning, she told me two things: "You'd better be punctual, and you'd better ask questions. Nobody here expects you to know everything, but we do expect you to say when you don't."
>
> The office works on a hybrid model. Interns are in the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and the rest of the time we work from home, provided that we're available online between ten and four. Dana says that I can work flexibly as long as my tasks are finished on time. But if I'm going to miss a deadline, I have to tell her at least a day before. Otherwise, as she put it, "we have a problem."
>
> So far, I've mostly been checking measurements in spreadsheets and helping with paperwork, which is less exciting than designing bridges. But I've already found two mistakes in an old report. Dana didn't say much, but the next day she gave me a slightly bigger task, which I think was her way of saying thank you.
>
> I've also learned a lot about office life. Between you and me, there are a couple of colleagues who argue about the kitchen schedule every single day. It's petty, but it's sort of funny to watch. One awkward moment: on Thursday, I joined a conference call and forgot to turn off my camera while I was eating a sandwich. Nobody said anything, but next time I'm going to bring a less messy lunch, just in case.
>
> Dad, you'll be glad to know that I always carry a notebook. Mom, you'll be glad to know that I'm sleeping eight hours. Well, seven or so.
>
> Love, Tracy
>
> P.S. Owen says hi. He's an intern at a software company across the river, so we have lunch together on Thursdays.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Lời nhờ gián tiếp (`I was wondering if you could…`, `Could you…?`) lịch sự hơn câu hỏi thẳng. Câu hỏi chức năng hay hỏi kiểu câu này.
- `had better` mạnh hơn `should`, gần như cảnh báo. `provided that` = `as long as` = chỉ khi; `otherwise` = nếu không thì.
- Ngữ đồng vị (`Dana Okafor, a bridge engineer with…,`) trả lời luôn câu hỏi "Who is…?".
- Câu đùa giữa người thân (`Don't let it go to your head`) thường vừa khen vừa trêu. Đừng chọn phương án "giận dữ".
