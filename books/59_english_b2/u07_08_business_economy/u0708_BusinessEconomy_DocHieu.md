## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 7) — Kế hoạch 12 tháng của Chloe Bennett Design

> **Chloe Bennett Design: Plan for the Next Twelve Months**
>
> Prepared by: Chloe Bennett, April
>
> Where we are now
>
> When I started out as a freelancer sixteen months ago, I worked alone in a corner of my apartment with three clients and a lot of hope. Today the business has fourteen regular clients, and our revenue for the first three months of this year was almost twice as high as in the same period last year. By June, I will have been running the studio for a year and a half, and I can no longer do everything myself.
>
> Our first hire
>
> In May, I am going to employ my first designer, Ruby, who has been working at Harriet's café while she finished her design course. She will start part-time, and by the end of the summer she will have taken over our social media and the smaller projects, so that I can focus on bigger clients. It's worth paying a little more for someone I trust; there's no point in saving money if the work isn't good.
>
> The bakery contract
>
> Our biggest client, a bakery in Oregon that now has six shops, has asked us to design new packaging for all of its products. The contract is worth $18,000 over ten months. In our first meeting, they asked for a 20% discount. I didn't want to back out of the deal, but I wasn't ready to settle for a price that would not even cover Ruby's wages. In the end, both sides made a concession: a 10% discount, in exchange for a two-year contract and permission to show the work in our portfolio.
>
> Risks
>
> A small business can go from busy to empty very quickly. If the bakery contract ended, we would lose about 40% of our revenue. To reduce that risk, by December we will have found at least three new clients, so that no single client accounts for more than a quarter of our income.
>
> Looking ahead
>
> I'm not planning to scale up too fast. A small studio that does excellent work is better than a big one that does average work. But I do feel that it's time to stop calling this a "freelance job". It's a company now.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 8) — Lạm phát giải thích qua giá một tách cà phê

> **Why Does My Coffee Cost More? Inflation, Explained**
>
> Three years ago, a cup of coffee at Harriet's on Maple Street cost $3.00. Today it costs $3.75, a rise of 25 percent. "I kept my prices the same for as long as I could," says Harriet Brooks, who has run the café for more than a decade. "But when the price of beans shot up, I had no choice."
>
> What Harriet experienced has a name: inflation, the rate at which prices in general rise over time. A little inflation is considered normal, and many economists see about 2 percent a year as healthy. In 2022, however, inflation in the United States rose sharply, reaching about 9 percent in the middle of the year, its highest level in four decades.
>
> Why do prices rise? Economists usually point to two kinds of causes. Sometimes the cost of making or moving goods goes up: bad weather destroys crops, fuel becomes more expensive, or shipping is delayed, and businesses pass the higher costs on to their customers. Coffee is a good example. In early 2025, after long dry periods in Brazil and Vietnam, the world's two biggest coffee producers, the price of coffee beans on world markets reached a record high. At other times, prices rise because people want to buy more than businesses can supply. When demand grows faster than supply, sellers can charge more.
>
> Not every price rises at the same speed. Over the same three years, Harriet says, the price that she pays for beans roughly doubled, while milk went up by about 15 percent, and her rent rose by far the least. Inflation matters because it changes what your money can buy. If prices rise by 5 percent but your pay rises by only 3 percent, you are, in effect, poorer, even though the number on your paycheck is higher. That is why economists look not only at prices, but also at wages.
>
> Price rises tend to level off eventually, but prices rarely come down to where they were before. "People ask me if the price will go back to three dollars," Harriet says. "I tell them honestly: probably not. But the conversation at the counter is still free."

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `will have + V3` = tới mốc tương lai thì xong việc; `will have been + V-ing for…` = tới mốc đó đã làm được bao lâu.
- Số liệu: `twice as high as`, `roughly doubled`, `by far the least`. Câu hỏi hay tráo các con số giữa các mặt hàng.
- Bài giải thích kinh tế thường chia nguyên nhân thành loại. Câu hỏi áp dụng yêu cầu xếp ví dụ vào đúng loại.
- Câu nói cuối của nhân vật thường cho biết thái độ. Đọc cả vế đùa sau dấu chấm.
