## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 25) — Ethan kể về cuộc chạy 50 km đầu tiên

> **My First 50K: What Thirty-One Miles Taught Me**
>
> Written by: Ethan Hayes
>
> Any race that is longer than a marathon is called an ultramarathon. Mine was a 50-kilometer trail race in the mountains east of San Diego, which is about thirty-one miles of dirt, rocks and hills. I had been training for five months, mostly early in the morning before my shifts at the fire station.
>
> The first ten miles felt easy, which, as experienced runners will tell you, is a dangerous feeling. I started too fast, and by mile fifteen, my legs were already complaining. On the long climb in the middle of the race, dozens of runners passed me. I didn't mind them passing me; I minded my own body refusing to go any faster. I regret not training on more hills. Running on the flat streets near the beach was pleasant, but it did not prepare me for climbing for two hours.
>
> At the aid station at mile twenty, a volunteer handed me a cup of soup and a boiled potato covered in salt, and I have never eaten anything so delicious in my life. The last eleven miles were a mixture of walking, running and talking to myself. What kept me going was the thought of the finish line, where Leah had promised to be waiting. Since our difficult winter last year, we have kept one evening a week that belongs only to us, and somehow she had moved her shift so that she could be there.
>
> I finished in seven hours and twelve minutes, near the back of the field. I can't imagine Leah not being there. She was holding a sign that said "I'm so proud of you (and also: why?)". It's a fair question. I think the answer is that, for one long day, nothing else mattered except the next step. Will I do it again? Ask me when my legs work again.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 26) — Truyền thống thay đổi theo thời gian và theo từng nhà

> **Traditions Are Younger Than You Think**
>
> Many families believe that their holiday traditions have stayed the same for hundreds of years. In fact, traditions change all the time, often faster than we notice.
>
> Take the American Thanksgiving meal. Most people imagine that the famous harvest meal in Plymouth in 1621 looked much like a modern one. But the only written description of it mentions deer and wild birds, and historians are not sure that turkey was served at all. Many of the dishes that families now consider "traditional" became common only in the last century or so.
>
> Traditions also differ widely from family to family. In some families, the meal would not be complete without sweet potato pie; in others, there are tamales, rice dishes or noodles next to the turkey, depending on where grandparents grew up. "My grandmother would make twelve dishes, and nobody was allowed to help," one woman in her sixties told us. "Now we each bring one. It is less perfect, but nobody falls asleep from exhaustion."
>
> Some changes are completely new. "Friendsgiving", a meal shared with friends instead of family, was hardly known before the 2000s, but many young people now celebrate it, especially those who live far from home. When we asked two college roommates about it, one said, "I miss my family's dinner." "So do I," said the other, "but I wouldn't miss this one either."
>
> Why do traditions change? Usually for practical reasons: families move, people marry into other families with their own customs, and new members bring new dishes. Some older people feel that something is being lost. Others see it differently: a tradition that never changed, they say, would be one that nobody needed any more. Perhaps what really stays the same is not the food on the table, but the people who sit around it.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `I don't mind them passing me` = không bận tâm việc họ vượt; `I regret not training` = tiếc vì đã không tập. Câu hỏi nghĩa hay tráo ai làm, làm hay không làm.
- `would + V` trong lời kể về quá khứ = thói quen lặp lại, giống `used to`. Đừng nhầm với câu điều kiện.
- `So do I` đồng ý với câu khẳng định; `Neither do I` đồng ý với câu phủ định.
- Bài về phong tục: chú ý các cụm khoanh vùng (`in some families`, `depending on where grandparents grew up`). Đáp án đúng không bao giờ quy chụp cả một nước.
