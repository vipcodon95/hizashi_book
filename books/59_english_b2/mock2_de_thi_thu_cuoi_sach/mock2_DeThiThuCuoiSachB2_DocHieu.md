> **Nhân vật:** Helena Brooks, Nathan Brooks, Tina Carter, Amy Brooks · tháng 10, năm 7
>
> **Mục tiêu:** Đề thi thử 40 câu, làm trong 60 phút (theo format đọc VSTEP bậc 4: 4 bài, 330–420 từ mỗi bài). Đề có 4 phần × 10 câu, khó dần: nội quy và sự kiện ở công viên bỏ túi → bài viết của Helena về nghề xây cầu → bài khoa học về tiếng cười → bài quan điểm về việc học lập trình thời AI. Bài đọc dùng ngữ pháp của cả khoá B2: provided that, đảo ngữ Had we…, being + V3, bị động tiếp diễn, be better off + V-ing, may well, not because … but because …

## Phần 1 — Nội quy và sự kiện mùa hè ở công viên bỏ túi

> **Pine Street Pocket Park: Rules and Summer Events**
>
> Since it opened in January, our little park behind the Pine Street Library has been used by hundreds of neighbors every week. To keep it pleasant for everyone, please read the following.
>
> Opening hours
>
> The park is open from 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. every day. The gate next to the library is locked at night, but the ramp entrance on Maple Street is always open for emergency services.
>
> Park rules
>
> Dogs are welcome provided that they are kept on a leash and their owners clean up after them. Please do not climb the young trees. They were planted by volunteers only eight months ago and are still easily damaged. Music is allowed at a volume that does not disturb other visitors. Bicycles must be walked, not ridden, inside the park. The little free library in the southeast corner works on trust: take a book, leave a book, and please don't take more than two at a time.
>
> Summer events
>
> Story mornings for children aged 4 to 8, led by Tina Carter and Amy Brooks, take place every Saturday at 10 a.m. Children under 6 must be with an adult. Music in the Park takes place on the first Friday of each month at 6:30 p.m. Local musicians of all levels are invited to play; beginners are especially welcome. The Bird Count, run by the wetland group, will visit on Sunday, July 9, to show children how to identify the birds that use the park's native trees.
>
> The park was built by neighbors and is cared for by neighbors. If you notice a problem, such as a broken bench or litter that has not been collected, please report it on the Maple Street Neighbors Chat, or leave a note at the library's front desk.

## Phần 2 — Bài viết của Helena: xây cầu đã dạy tôi điều gì về con người

> **What Building Bridges Taught Me About People**
>
> Written by: Helena Brooks
>
> When I started studying engineering, I believed that my job would be about concrete, steel and numbers. Twenty years later, I am convinced that it is mostly about people. A bridge, after all, is a promise that two places will be connected, and promises only matter to the people who depend on them.
>
> The first lesson came early. On my first project, I designed what I thought was a perfect pedestrian bridge. It was strong, cheap and elegant. Then an elderly woman at a community meeting asked me where she could sit down in the middle of the bridge, and I realized that I had never once imagined someone like her using it. We added two benches. They are, I am told, the most popular part of the bridge.
>
> The second lesson is that people are rarely against a project; they are against being ignored. In Valley Springs, where we replaced an old wooden bridge, some residents were angry at first about the noise and the traffic. Had we simply carried on, the anger would have grown. Instead, we held a meeting every month, explained what would happen next, and changed our working hours during the harvest. By the end, a farmer was bringing us peaches every Friday.
>
> The third lesson is the hardest to accept: the best work is often invisible. Nobody notices a bridge that works. They notice it only when it fails. Engineers have to learn to be proud of things that nobody will ever thank them for.
>
> My daughter recently asked me whether I wished I had chosen a more exciting job. I told her the truth: I get to spend my life making it easier for people to reach each other. I can't think of anything more exciting than that.

## Phần 3 — Bài khoa học về tiếng cười

> **Why We Laugh**
>
> Most of us assume that we laugh because something is funny. Research suggests that this is only a small part of the story.
>
> In the 1990s, the psychologist Robert Provine and his students listened to people laughing in shopping centers, on college campuses and on city streets, and wrote down what had been said just before each laugh. To their surprise, only a small share of the laughter, fewer than one laugh in five, followed anything that looked like a joke. Most laughter came after ordinary comments, such as "I'll see you guys later" or "Are you sure?".
>
> Provine also found that we are around thirty times more likely to laugh when we are with other people than when we are alone. This has led many scientists to argue that laughter is first of all a social signal: a way of telling others that we are friendly, that we understand them and that we belong to the same group.
>
> We are not the only animals that laugh. Apes make soft, laugh-like sounds when they play and tickle each other, and in the 1990s, a scientist discovered that young rats make high-pitched chirps when they are tickled, sounds that are far too high for human ears to hear without special equipment. The rats seemed to enjoy it, too: they kept coming back to the scientist's hand for more.
>
> Laughing together also seems to be good for us. In one study, people who watched a comedy in a group were able to tolerate more pain afterward than people who watched a less funny program, possibly because laughter makes the brain release chemicals that reduce pain and make us feel close to others. So the next time you laugh at a friend's terrible joke, don't feel embarrassed. You are not really judging the joke. You are telling your friend that you are on the same side.

## Phần 4 — Bài quan điểm: có ai cũng cần học lập trình?

> **Does Everyone Still Need to Learn to Code?**
>
> Ten years ago, the advice was everywhere: learn to code. Schools added programming classes, and parents sent their children to coding camps in the summer. Today, AI tools can write working code in seconds. In 2024, one of the largest technology companies said that more than a quarter of its new code was being generated by AI. So is the old advice now out of date?
>
> Some argue that it is. If a machine can turn a plain English description into a working program, they say, students would be better off spending their time on skills that machines are less good at, such as writing, negotiating or caring for people. Learning the exact rules of a programming language, which may well change in five years, seems less useful than ever.
>
> Others strongly disagree. AI tools make mistakes, and someone has to notice them. A person who has never written code cannot easily tell whether a program is correct, safe or even doing what it was asked to do. Nathan Brooks, a software team lead in San Diego, put it simply: "The tools write more code than ever, but my team spends more time than ever reading it. You can't check what you don't understand."
>
> In my view, both sides are asking the wrong question. The most valuable thing that coding classes teach was never the code itself. It was a way of thinking: breaking a big problem into small steps, testing an idea, finding out why it failed and trying again. These skills are useful for a nurse, a farmer or a designer, and they will not go out of date.
>
> So yes, students should still learn some coding, not because everyone will become a programmer, but because a future full of machines will need people who understand how to give them clear instructions, and how to question the answers that come back.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 11 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 3 khoảng 16 phút, Phần 4 khoảng 18 phút.
- **Thông báo:** câu áp dụng luôn hỏi điều kiện (`under 6`, `provided that`). So con số trong câu hỏi với con số trong bài.
- **Bài luận cá nhân:** người viết đánh số bài học (`The first lesson`, `The second lesson`, `The third lesson`). Câu hỏi ẩn dụ (`a bridge is a promise`) hỏi người viết thật sự muốn nói gì về con người.
- **Bài khoa học:** tách các nghiên cứu (Provine, chuột, phim hài) và kết quả của từng nghiên cứu. Từ rào đón (`possibly`, `seem`) cho biết đâu là giả thuyết.
- **Bài quan điểm:** cấu trúc thường là câu hỏi → hai phía → ý kiến người viết (`In my view`). Câu "not because X, but because Y" cho biết Y mới là lý do thật.
