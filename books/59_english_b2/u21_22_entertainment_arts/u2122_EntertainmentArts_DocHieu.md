## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 21) — Bài điểm vở kịch sân sau của Tina và Amy

> **Review: The Dragon Who Was Afraid of the Dark**
>
> Maple Street Neighborhood News · by Corinne Whitfield
>
> I have reviewed shows in real theaters for twenty years, but I have rarely enjoyed an evening as much as I enjoyed Saturday night in the Brooks family's backyard. About forty neighbors sat on folding chairs, blankets and, in one case, an upside-down bucket, to watch The Dragon Who Was Afraid of the Dark. The play was written by Tina Carter and directed by Amy Brooks, and it was performed by six children from the street.
>
> The story is simple: a young dragon who can breathe fire is too frightened to leave his cave at night, until a small owl teaches him that the dark is just the day with its eyes closed. It is such a lovely idea that several adults in the audience were seen wiping their eyes. The owl's song near the end, played on the ukulele by Megan Carter, was so sweet a moment that nobody wanted it to finish.
>
> Not everything went to plan. The dragon's costume, made from cardboard by Helena Brooks, lost its tail in the first scene, and the lights, which were controlled by Trevor Carter, went off at the wrong moment twice. The owl's first speech was perhaps too long a speech for an eight-year-old, and the actor had to be helped by a loud voice from behind the curtain. And Biscuit, the Carters' dog, who had been given a small part as "the world's laziest dragon", fell asleep on stage and could not be woken up.
>
> Strangely, the problems made the evening better. Every time something went wrong, the audience laughed, the actors laughed, and the show simply carried on. Tickets were free, but donations were collected for the animal shelter on Harbor Street, and $212 was raised. I am told that a second play is already being written. If so, I will be there, and I will bring my own bucket.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 22) — Công việc của người phục chế tranh

> **Saving Old Masters: The Quiet Work of Art Conservators**
>
> As you know if you have ever looked closely at an old painting in a museum, time is not kind to art. Colors fade, paint cracks, and the protective layer of varnish that artists put on top slowly turns yellow, so that a bright blue sky can look green and gloomy after a few hundred years.
>
> The people who care for these works are called conservators, and their job is a mixture of science, art and great patience. Before they touch a painting, they study it with the same tools as doctors use: X-rays and infrared cameras can show what lies under the surface, such as earlier sketches or parts that the artist painted over. Tiny samples of paint, often smaller than a grain of sand, are analyzed to find out which materials were used.
>
> One of the most important rules of modern conservation is that every change should be reversible. If old varnish is removed, the new varnish must be one that a future conservator can take off without harming the original paint. Missing areas are filled in so carefully that they cannot be seen from a normal distance, but they can always be found under special light. The idea is simple: conservators should never pretend that their work is the artist's.
>
> Some projects are now done in public. Since 2019, the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam has been studying and restoring Rembrandt's huge painting The Night Watch inside a glass room, so that visitors can watch the experts at work. The team even used computer programs to recreate parts of the painting that had been cut off in the 1700s, so that people could see it as close to the way it once looked as possible.
>
> Conservation is slow. A single painting can take years, and much of the work is done with brushes as thin as a hair. But without it, many of the paintings that we travel across the world to see would simply disappear, one crack at a time.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `such a lovely idea that…` / `so sweet a moment that…` = … tới mức mà. `too long a speech for…` = quá … so với.
- Bài phê bình: tìm chỗ người viết khen (`as much as`, `so sweet`) và chỗ chê (`Not everything went to plan`). Câu NOT hay hỏi vào danh sách trục trặc.
- Bài kiến thức: một quy tắc (`every change should be reversible`) thường được giải thích bằng ví dụ ngay sau. Đọc ví dụ để hiểu quy tắc.
- Câu kết có hình ảnh (`one crack at a time`): dịch thành ý thường (dần dần, từng chút một).
