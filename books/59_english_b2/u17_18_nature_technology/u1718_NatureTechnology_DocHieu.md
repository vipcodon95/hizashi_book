## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 17) — Nhật ký quan sát chim di cư của Tina

> **Tina's Bird Journal: The Travelers at the Bay**
>
> Saturday, October 2
>
> Today was my first morning at the bird count at the wetlands on the south side of San Diego Bay. There was a thick morning mist when we arrived, and our leader, a retired science teacher called Mrs. Delgado, said that we had to be completely silent. For a long time, I couldn't make out anything at all. Then I heard something call across the water, and slowly the mist lifted.
>
> Hundreds of small brown birds were running along the edge of the mud, all moving together like one animal. Mrs. Delgado said that they were western sandpipers, and that some of them had flown all the way from Alaska. I watched one of them stop, push its beak into the mud and pull out a tiny worm. It was gone in a second.
>
> Mrs. Delgado explained that San Diego Bay is on the Pacific Flyway, a kind of highway in the sky that birds follow between the far north and South America. Every fall, millions of birds stop at wetlands like this one to rest and eat before they continue their journey. "Think of it as a gas station," she said. "If the gas stations disappear, the drivers don't make it home."
>
> Saturday, October 9
>
> This week, I counted forty-two different species, and I finally saw an osprey dive. I saw it fall out of the sky, hit the water and rise again with a fish in its feet. Everybody cheered, which Mrs. Delgado said was "very unscientific, but understandable". Our counts are sent to scientists, who use them to see how bird numbers are changing. Mrs. Delgado told us that North America has lost almost three billion birds since 1970. I can't stop thinking about that number. I used to want to be a vet. Now I think that I might want to be the person who counts.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 18) — Khi chiếc loa thông minh về sống cùng gia đình

> **The Speaker in the Kitchen: Smart Assistants and Family Life**
>
> In many American homes, there is now a small device on the kitchen counter that plays music, reads recipes aloud, answers homework questions and turns off the lights. It is estimated that about a third of households in the United States own at least one smart speaker. For busy families, the appeal is obvious. It is easy to see why a parent with flour on her hands finds it useful to set a timer just by speaking.
>
> But these devices raise questions that many families have not thought about. To hear their wake word, smart speakers must listen all the time. The companies that make them say that recordings only begin after the word is heard. However, devices have been known to wake up by mistake and record private conversations, and some users are unaware that recordings may be stored and, in some cases, reviewed by people working for the company. Most speakers allow users to delete their recordings, but few people ever check the settings.
>
> Children raise a different question. Young children are thought to see smart speakers as something between a toy and a person, and some parents find it worrying that their children shout orders at the device without ever saying "please". In response, some companies have added settings that thank children when they ask politely. Others worry less about manners and more about homework: if a speaker can answer any question instantly, will children still learn how to find things out for themselves?
>
> None of this means that families should throw their speakers away. It is clear, though, that a device which listens in the kitchen deserves at least as much thought as one that we carry in our pockets. Experts suggest a few simple habits: check the privacy settings, turn off the microphone during private conversations, and talk with children about what the device is and isn't. A smart speaker can be a helpful assistant. It should not become the quietest member of the family, always listening and never asked.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `see / hear + O + V` = thấy, nghe trọn hành động; `+ V-ing` = thấy đang diễn ra giữa chừng. Câu hỏi thường hỏi đúng sự khác biệt này.
- Phép so sánh của nhân vật (`Think of it as a gas station`): tìm câu giải thích ngay sau để hiểu điểm giống nhau.
- Bị động tường thuật (`It is estimated that`, `are thought to`) cho biết đây là ý kiến hay ước tính chung, chưa phải sự thật tuyệt đối.
- Câu kết bài quan điểm thường dùng ẩn dụ. Hãy dịch ẩn dụ thành một lời khuyên cụ thể.
