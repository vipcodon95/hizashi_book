## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 3) — Chuyên mục của Nathan: kiểm chứng một bức ảnh lan truyền

> **Ten Minutes Before You Share**
>
> Nathan Brooks, "Tech Corner", Maple Street Neighbors' Newsletter, February
>
> Last month, a dramatic photo went into circulation on our neighborhood group chat. It supposedly showed the Pine Street Library under a meter of brown water after the January storm, with books floating out of the front door. Within an hour, it had been shared fourteen times, including by my own mother, who added three crying faces and the words "Is this true??"
>
> It was not true. The photo had been made by an image generator, and it took me about ten minutes to find that out. I want to explain how, because the method matters more than this particular photo.
>
> A few years ago, the standard advice was to look for mistakes in the picture itself: a hand with six fingers, letters on a sign that make no sense. That advice is now out of date. Image generators have improved so quickly that, arguably, nobody can reliably spot a fake just by staring at it.
>
> A better method comes from a well-known 2017 study by researchers at Stanford University. They asked three groups, namely professional historians, university students and professional fact-checkers, to judge how credible various websites were. The historians and students read each page carefully from top to bottom, and they were often fooled by official-looking logos. The fact-checkers did something different: they left the page almost at once and opened new tabs to find out what other sources said about it. This is called "lateral reading", and the fact-checkers reached better conclusions in a fraction of the time.
>
> So I read laterally. First, who posted the photo? The account had been created only two weeks earlier, and it had posted dozens of similar flood pictures from cities all over the world. That an account so new had produced so much drama told me a great deal. Second, what did credible local sources say? Not one local news site had reported a flood, and the library's own page had posted a photo that morning of its staff, dry and smiling, opening the doors at nine.
>
> I am not saying this to make anyone feel foolish. My mother shared the photo because she loves that library, and that is precisely why such fakes work: they are designed to make us feel before we think. To my mind, the answer is not to trust nothing, which is just another way of being fooled. It is to take ten minutes, open a new tab, and ask who else is saying this. If nobody credible is, the photo can wait.

### Câu hỏi

**1. What did the viral photo supposedly show?**
*(Bức ảnh lan truyền được cho là chụp cảnh gì?)*

- A. Nathan's house flooded after the January storm *(nhà Nathan bị ngập sau cơn bão tháng Một)*
- B. the Maple Street Library covered in snow after the January storm *(thư viện Maple Street phủ đầy tuyết sau cơn bão tháng Một)*
- C. the Pine Street Library flooded after the January storm *(thư viện Pine Street ngập sau cơn bão tháng Một)*
- D. a library closing for good after it ran out of money *(một thư viện đóng cửa hẳn vì hết tiền)*

**2. Why does Nathan say he wants to explain how he checked the photo?**
*(Vì sao Nathan nói anh muốn giải thích cách anh kiểm tra bức ảnh?)*

- A. because the method is more important than this one photo *(vì phương pháp quan trọng hơn chính bức ảnh này)*
- B. because his mother asked him to show everyone how he found the photo *(vì mẹ anh nhờ anh chỉ cho mọi người cách anh tìm ra bức ảnh)*
- C. because he made the photo himself to show how easy it is *(vì chính anh làm ra bức ảnh để cho thấy việc đó dễ thế nào)*
- D. because the library paid him to write about the flood story *(vì thư viện trả tiền để anh viết về chuyện lũ)*

**3. According to Nathan, why is the old advice about six fingers and strange signs out of date?**
*(Theo Nathan, vì sao lời khuyên cũ về sáu ngón tay và biển hiệu lạ đã lỗi thời?)*

- A. because people no longer share photos on the neighborhood group chat *(vì người ta không còn chia sẻ ảnh trên nhóm chat của khu phố)*
- B. because fake photos have become so easy to recognize that nobody needs to check them any more *(vì ảnh giả đã trở nên dễ nhận ra đến mức không ai cần kiểm tra nữa)*
- C. because signs and hands are now hidden by the websites that carry the photos *(vì biển hiệu và bàn tay giờ bị các trang web đăng ảnh che đi)*
- D. because image generators have improved so much that staring at a picture is unreliable *(vì công cụ tạo ảnh tiến bộ tới mức nhìn chằm chằm vào ảnh không còn đáng tin)*

**4. How did the fact-checkers in the Stanford study judge websites?**
*(Những người kiểm chứng tin trong nghiên cứu Stanford đánh giá trang web thế nào?)*

- A. They read every page carefully from top to bottom before deciding. *(Họ đọc kỹ mỗi trang từ trên xuống dưới rồi mới quyết định.)*
- B. They quickly left the page to see what other sources said about it. *(Họ nhanh chóng rời trang để xem nguồn khác nói gì về nó.)*
- C. They looked carefully at the logos to decide which sites were official. *(Họ nhìn kỹ các logo để quyết định trang nào là chính thức.)*
- D. They asked a group of historians to help them judge each website. *(Họ nhờ một nhóm nhà sử học giúp đánh giá từng trang web.)*

**5. What can be inferred about the historians and students in the study?**
*(Có thể suy ra gì về các nhà sử học và sinh viên trong nghiên cứu?)*

- A. Their careful reading of a single page did not protect them from being fooled. *(Việc đọc kỹ một trang không giúp họ khỏi bị đánh lừa.)*
- B. They were faster than the fact-checkers at reaching a conclusion. *(Họ đưa ra kết luận nhanh hơn người kiểm chứng tin.)*
- C. They refused to take part once they saw the websites. *(Họ từ chối tham gia khi thấy các trang web.)*
- D. They knew almost nothing about history or about how websites work. *(Họ hầu như không biết gì về lịch sử hay cách các trang web hoạt động.)*

**6. What does the sentence beginning "That an account so new" suggest Nathan concluded?**
*(Câu bắt đầu bằng "That an account so new" cho thấy Nathan đã kết luận gì?)*

- A. The account belonged to a well-known local news site. *(Tài khoản thuộc về một trang tin địa phương có tiếng.)*
- B. The account was very popular because it posted so many photos. *(Tài khoản rất nổi tiếng vì đăng rất nhiều ảnh.)*
- C. The account was probably not a trustworthy source. *(Tài khoản có lẽ không phải nguồn đáng tin.)*
- D. The account had good photos taken by a professional. *(Tài khoản có những bức ảnh đẹp do người chuyên nghiệp chụp.)*

**7. Which piece of evidence most clearly showed that the photo was false?**
*(Bằng chứng nào cho thấy rõ nhất bức ảnh là giả?)*

- A. The photo had been shared fourteen times in less than an hour. *(Bức ảnh đã được chia sẻ mười bốn lần trong chưa đầy một giờ.)*
- B. Harriet added crying faces and asked whether the photo was true. *(Bà Harriet thêm biểu tượng mặt khóc và hỏi bức ảnh có thật không.)*
- C. No local news site reported a flood, and the library's page showed it open and dry. *(Không trang tin địa phương nào đưa tin lũ, và trang của thư viện cho thấy nó mở cửa, khô ráo.)*
- D. The storm happened in January, and the photo showed a meter of water. *(Cơn bão xảy ra vào tháng Một và bức ảnh cho thấy nước sâu một mét.)*

**8. Why does Nathan say he is "not saying this to make anyone feel foolish"?**
*(Vì sao Nathan nói anh "không nói ra điều này để khiến ai thấy mình ngớ ngẩn"?)*

- A. because he also shared the photo at first and now feels embarrassed about doing so *(vì lúc đầu anh cũng chia sẻ bức ảnh và giờ thấy xấu hổ về việc đó)*
- B. because he wants to be kind and explain why fakes work on people who care *(vì anh muốn tử tế và giải thích vì sao ảnh giả đánh trúng những người biết quan tâm)*
- C. because he is afraid his mother will be angry with him for writing about it *(vì anh sợ mẹ sẽ giận anh vì viết về chuyện này)*
- D. because the library asked him to be polite to people who shared the photo *(vì thư viện nhờ anh lịch sự với những người đã chia sẻ bức ảnh)*

**9. What is Nathan's position on trusting information online?**
*(Quan điểm của Nathan về việc tin thông tin trên mạng là gì?)*

- D. We should stop using group chats, since fakes spread there so quickly. *(Ta nên bỏ nhóm chat, vì ảnh giả lan ở đó rất nhanh.)*

> Option one is a small apartment in a high-rise downtown, situated about ten minutes from the hospital. The layout is clever, the views are wonderful, and with both of us working night shifts, a short commute matters enormously. The building also has a gym and a rooftop garden, although the monthly fees that the occupants pay for them are almost as high as some people's rent. The problem is the price. Even the smallest unit would take almost all of our savings, thereby leaving us with no buffer at all if something went wrong.
>
> Option two is Ethan's favorite: an older house in the suburbs, far more spacious, with a garden and a garage. It needs serious refurbishment, but Ethan, who has never fixed anything in his life, is sure he can patch up the plumbing himself. His main argument is that we would finally have room for a dog and, perhaps one day, for children, which is hard to argue with. I reminded him of what happened when your dad tried that. More seriously, the drive to the hospital is forty minutes on a good day, and with the freeway clogged up every morning, it could easily be an hour. After a twelve-hour night shift, an hour in traffic is not a commute; it is a danger.
>
> Option three came from your parents, which surprised us both. Your dad suggested turning their old garage into a small cottage for us, the work involved being, in his words, "a fun project for the summer". It is a lovely offer, and I said I would think about it. But I have lived downstairs from your family for seven years, and I am not sure that a married couple should still be borrowing your mom's frying pan.
>
> So here is where we have ended up, for now. We are going to rent a two-bedroom apartment near the hospital for a year and save as much as we can. That being said, the spreadsheet has a new tab called "Houses (one day)", which Ethan updates every night.
>
> Your turn: how is the apartment hunt in Washington going? Love, Leah

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `supposedly`, `allegedly`, `reportedly` báo hiệu người viết **chưa tin** điều đi sau. Đây là gợi ý sớm cho câu hỏi thái độ.
- Câu hỏi cấu trúc bài: tìm các câu chuyển ý (`A better method comes from…`, `So I read laterally`, `To my mind`). Mỗi câu là đầu một bước lập luận.
- Email so sánh phương án: mỗi phương án có ưu → nhược (`The problem is…`). Câu hỏi nhược điểm lấy ở câu sau từ chuyển ý.
- `That being said` / `Having said that` = ý ngược lại sắp tới. Câu hỏi hàm ý thường nằm ngay sau cụm này.
