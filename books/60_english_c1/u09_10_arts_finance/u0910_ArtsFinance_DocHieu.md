## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 9) — Bài phê bình triển lãm tranh đầu tiên của Helena

> **Review: "Bridges and Small Things" by Helena Brooks**
>
> By Jordan Wells, Weekend Arts page. At the gallery of Pine Street Library until September 30. Free entry.
>
> Helena Brooks is not, by her own admission, a professional artist. She is a civil engineer who has spent more than fifteen years designing bridges and painting on weekends, and this exhibition of twenty-four works is her first. I went expecting the kind of pleasant, slightly amateur show that libraries often host. What I found was more interesting, and more uneven, than that. It is, in other words, a debut, and it deserves to be judged as one: not by asking whether it is perfect, but by asking whether it shows a voice of its own.
>
> The small paintings are the heart of the show. A pair of child's sneakers left on a back step. A cup of coffee going cold on a windowsill. The silhouette of an old man feeding birds in a park at dusk. So quiet are these pictures that you could easily walk past them, and yet each one rewards a second look. The light is handled with an engineer's precision, and the mood is reminiscent of Edward Hopper's empty streets, though warmer and less lonely.
>
> Beside each painting hangs a short poem by the artist. This I did not expect, and I was ready to dislike it. In fact, the poems are often wry and unconventional, and they give the show a gentle sense of humor. Under the cold coffee, for example, she has written: "Someone was called away. / The coffee waited. / It always does."
>
> As for the large panoramic paintings of bridges that give the show half of its title, they are, I am sorry to say, the weakest work here. They are technically impressive, every cable and beam in its place, but they feel like drawings for a client rather than paintings for a viewer. One of them, a huge sunset over the new bridge at Valley Springs, is frankly showy, with colors that need toning down. Next to the small works, most of them seem lackluster, and the open sketchbooks displayed beside them, full of quick pencil studies of the same bridges, are far more alive. It is as if the engineer and the painter had not quite agreed who was in charge.
>
> Is this, then, a show worth visiting? Without question. Uneven first exhibitions are often more alive than polished later ones, and the small works here evoke something that much bigger art often misses: the feeling that ordinary moments are worth noticing. My advice to the artist would be to trust the small things. My advice to readers is simpler: go, and take your time. And isn't that, in the end, what these paintings are asking of us?

### Câu hỏi

**1. What did the critic expect before visiting the exhibition?**
*(Trước khi tới xem, nhà phê bình đã đoán điều gì?)*

- A. a famous artist's latest work in a big city museum *(tác phẩm mới nhất của một hoạ sĩ nổi tiếng trong một bảo tàng lớn)*
- B. an exhibition about engineering and bridge design *(một triển lãm về kỹ thuật và thiết kế cầu)*

- D. a show that would be closed on the day he visited *(một triển lãm sẽ đóng cửa vào ngày ông đến)*

**2. What does the word "uneven" suggest about the exhibition?**

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 10) — Bản ghi nhớ của Nathan: ba phương án cho tiệm bên cạnh

> **Memo: What Should We Do With the Shop Next Door?**
>
> To: Harriet Brooks. From: Nathan. Date: August 26
>
> Mom, you asked me to put the numbers on paper before you decide about the old bakery next door, which has now been empty for more than a year. Here they are. I have tried to be honest rather than encouraging.
>
> Option A: expand the café into the shop. This would double the number of seats. The rent is $3,200 a month, and turning a bakery into a café would cost around $45,000, most of which we would have to borrow. If the extra seats were full at lunchtime, the loan would be paid off in about three years. But more seats do not automatically mean more customers, and in an economic downturn, a bigger café would lose money faster than a small one would. We would also need two more staff, which would increase our monthly expenditure substantially, and a second coffee machine alone would mean forking out another $9,000. The bakery closed for exactly that reason, as did the bookstore on the corner before it.
>
> Option B: open a shared kitchen. The bakery already has two large ovens and a professional kitchen, so the refurbishment would cost far less, about $12,000. Home cooks and small food businesses, such as the families who cooked for The Neighbors' Table two autumns ago, could rent the kitchen by the hour. Several have already asked whether such a place exists. The arrangement would also be mutually useful: the cooks would get affordable space, and the café would gain prospective customers every time someone came to collect their food. My estimation is that it would cover its costs within eighteen months if the kitchen were rented for just four hours a day. It would earn less than Option A at its best, but it would also lose much less at its worst.
>
> Option C: do nothing. I am including this seriously. The café makes a small but steady surplus, you are seventy-four, and you have nothing to prove to anyone. Saying no is not failure; it is a decision. Your savings are your buffer, and I would hate to see them squandered on a bigger room that nobody needed.
>
> My own view, for what it is worth, is that the question is not so much how to make the most money as what kind of place you want Harriet's to be in five years' time. Option A makes it bigger. ## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Bài phê bình C1 hiếm khi chỉ khen hoặc chỉ chê. Tìm cặp **mạnh nhất / yếu nhất** (`the heart of the show` ↔ `the weakest work here`) để trả lời câu hỏi kết luận.
- Đảo ngữ `So quiet are these pictures that…` nhấn mạnh tính chất; nghĩa thật thường nằm ở vế sau (`and yet each one rewards a second look`).
- Câu hỏi tu từ cuối bài (`isn't that…?`) **không hỏi**, mà khẳng định thông điệp chính.
- Bản ghi nhớ so sánh phương án: chú ý cặp `at its best` / `at its worst`, và câu `not so much A as B` cho biết người viết coi B mới là câu hỏi thật.
