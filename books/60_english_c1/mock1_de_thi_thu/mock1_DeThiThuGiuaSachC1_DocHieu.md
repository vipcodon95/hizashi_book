>

>
> **Mục tiêu:** Đề thi thử 40 câu, làm trong 60 phút (theo format đọc VSTEP bậc 5: 4 bài, 320–380 từ mỗi bài). ## Phần 2 — Bài khoa học: quạ nhớ mặt người

> **The Birds Who Never Forget a Face**
>
> In 2006, a group of researchers at the University of Washington in Seattle walked across their own campus wearing rubber masks. One mask showed the face of a cave man; the other showed an ordinary face that the researchers used for comparison. While wearing the cave-man mask, they caught seven wild crows, put small identification bands on their legs, and let them go. The birds were not hurt, but they were clearly not pleased.
>
> What happened next turned out to be far more interesting than the banding itself. Whenever a researcher walked across campus in the cave-man mask, crows would gather in the trees and "scold" the person, making loud, angry calls and sometimes diving towards them. The ordinary mask produced almost no reaction. The crows recognized the dangerous face even when it was partly hidden under a hat or worn upside down.
>
> Even more startling, crows that had never been caught joined in. Over the following years, the share of local crows that scolded the mask rose sharply, until the birds who reacted far outnumbered the seven that had actually been banded. The most plausible explanation is that crows learn from one another. A young bird that sees adults scolding a particular face will, it seems, learn to treat that face as a threat without ever having been harmed by it.
>
> Why would a bird need such a skill? Crows live for many years, often in the same area, and they share that area with humans who may feed them, ignore them or chase them away. For a crow, knowing which humans to be wary of is a matter of survival. Sharing that knowledge with the group is an efficient way to protect everyone, including birds that were not there when the danger first appeared.
>
> The study has influenced how many scientists think about animal intelligence, which used to be measured mainly by what a single animal could do alone. Crows suggest that intelligence can also be social: a kind of memory that belongs not to one bird but to the whole community. The lesson for humans is simple. If you live near crows, it may be wise to stay on good terms with them.

## Phần 3 — Truyện: Tracy phát hiện lỗi của chính mình

> **The Number on Page Fourteen**
>
> It was a quarter to six on a Friday, and most of the office at Brandt & Lowe had already gone home, when Tracy found the mistake. It was in her own calculation, on page fourteen of a report about a small pedestrian bridge, and the report had been sent to the client on Wednesday. She had used the wrong figure for the weight of the railings. The bridge would not fall down; she checked three times to make sure of that. But one of the beams was thinner than the rules required, and it would have to be changed.
>
> For a few minutes, she simply sat there. Nobody else had noticed. The client would not start building for another two months, and she could quietly correct the drawings next week and say nothing. Then she thought about Dana Okafor, who had signed off on the report because she trusted Tracy's numbers. She picked up the phone.
>
> **Tracy:** Dana, I'm sorry to call so late. I've found a mistake in the bridge report, and it's mine.
>
> There was a short silence.
>
> **Dana:** Is anyone in danger?
>
> **Tracy:** No. Construction doesn't start until July. But one beam needs to be bigger, and the client has already got the report.
>
> **Dana:** Then here is what we'll do. Send me your corrected calculation tonight, if you can, and I'll check it over the weekend. On Monday, you and I will call the client together, explain what happened and send a revised report.
>
> **Tracy:** I really am sorry.
>
> **Dana:** I know. Tracy, listen. Everyone in this profession makes mistakes. The ones I worry about are the people who hide them. The mistake was yours, but so was the decision to call me, and that is the part I will remember.
>
> On Monday, the call with the client took eleven minutes. The client thanked them for being open, and the revised report went out that afternoon. That evening, Tracy wrote a single line on a sticky note and put it on her screen, where it stayed for the rest of the year: "Check the railings."

## Phần 4 — Bài quan điểm của cô Lydia Park về tuần không điện thoại

> **One Week Without Phones: What My Classroom Taught Me**
>
> Lydia Park, English teacher, Westview High School
>
> In April, our school tried an experiment. For one week, every student placed their phone in a locked pouch at the start of the day and got it back at the final bell. The idea came from a group of students themselves, which is, I think, the most interesting part of the story.
>
> I expected anger. On Monday, I got it: two students told me, candidly, that the week was "basically a prison sentence". By Wednesday, however, something had changed. The lunch tables were louder. Students who usually sat in silence, each looking at a screen, were arguing about films, playing cards, and in one memorable case, holding a debate about whether a hot dog is a sandwich.
>
> In my own classes, the change was smaller than I had hoped but still real. Students who are easily distracted finished their reading more often, and fewer of them asked to leave the room. It would be dishonest, however, to pretend that the experiment had no costs. Several parents were worried that they could not reach their children in an emergency, and one student, who looks after a younger brother with a medical condition, found the week genuinely stressful. For her, the phone was not a distraction but a responsibility.
>
> So, should we lock phones away for good? I would argue that this is the wrong question. A permanent ban treats every student and every phone in the same way, as if a game and a message from a worried parent were the same thing. What the week showed me is that most students are perfectly able to live without their phones for a few hours; what they find hard is being the only one who does it.
>
> My proposal is modest: phone-free classrooms, phones allowed at lunch, and a simple way for families to reach the office in an emergency. Most importantly, the rules should be written with students, not just for them. After all, it was students who suggested the experiment in the first place.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 12 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 3 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 4 khoảng 18 phút.
- **Thông báo:** câu áp dụng luôn có một điều kiện (`under 10`, `must be booked`). So con số trong câu hỏi với con số trong bài.
- **Bài khoa học:** tách **điều quan sát được** (lũ quạ mắng mặt nạ) với **cách giải thích** (`The most plausible explanation`, `it seems`).
- **Truyện:** câu hỏi về tính cách lấy từ hành động và câu nói đầu tiên của nhân vật (`Is anyone in danger?`). Chi tiết cuối truyện (tờ giấy nhớ) thường hỏi điều nhân vật rút ra.
- **Bài quan điểm C1:** người viết hay **đặt lại câu hỏi** (`this is the wrong question`) trước khi nêu đề xuất. Câu cuối thường là bằng chứng cho đề xuất.
