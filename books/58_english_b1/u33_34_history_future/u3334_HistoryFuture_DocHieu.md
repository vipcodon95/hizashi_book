## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 33) — Trevor phỏng vấn bà Harriet về Maple Street ngày xưa

> **Maple Street in the 1970s: An Interview with Harriet Brooks**
>
> Written by: Trevor Carter, for Mr. Carter's history class
>
> For our history project, my dad, who is also my history teacher (unfortunately), asked us to interview someone who remembers life about fifty years ago. I chose Harriet Brooks, who moved to Maple Street in 1978 as a young teacher and has lived here ever since.
>
> When I asked her what had changed the most, she didn't hesitate: "The phones!" "We did have phones, of course," she said, "but ours was attached to the kitchen wall, and the whole family could hear every word that you said." If you wanted to talk to a friend privately, you had to pull the long cord into the closet and close the door.
>
> Kids spent much more time outdoors. "You went out after breakfast, and you came home when the street lights came on. Nobody knew exactly where you were, and nobody worried." Everything was close: children walked to school, to the grocery store and to the park. She admitted that parents today have good reasons to be more careful, "but I do think that children then were less afraid of things," she added.
>
> Some things haven't changed as much as I expected. The old fire station on Pine Street, which was built in 1925, is still there; it was restored in 2010 and is now the public library. And the big tree behind the library still has an inscription that some kids carved into it in 1979: "MAPLE STREET KIDS FOREVER." "I did tell them not to," Harriet said with a smile, "but I'm glad that they didn't listen."
>
> At the end, I asked her if life was better then. "Not better, not worse. Different," she said. "We wrote letters and waited weeks for an answer. You send a message and get a reply in seconds. But I do miss the waiting. It made every letter feel special."
>
> I expected this project to be boring. It wasn't. I've walked past that tree a thousand times, and I had never noticed the words.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 34) — Thành phố năm 2050

> **Cities in 2050: What Might Change?**
>
> Today, more than half of the people in the world live in cities. By 2050, experts estimate that the number will have grown to about two out of every three people. So what will these cities look like? Nobody can say with certainty, but city planners have a few clear ideas.
>
> First, cities are likely to become greener, partly to keep themselves cool. Concrete streets and dark roofs absorb heat, so a city can be several degrees hotter than the countryside around it. Many cities have already started planting trees and putting gardens on roofs, and by 2050, some of them will have changed their streets completely. In retrospect, some planners say that they should have started much earlier.
>
> Second, more people may live in what are called "fifteen-minute cities". The idea is simple: everything that you need every day, such as shops, schools, doctors and parks, should be within a fifteen-minute walk or bike ride from your home. Supporters say that this would mean less traffic and cleaner air, while others worry that it won't work in places where homes are far apart.
>
> Third, buildings will probably produce more of their own energy, with solar panels on their roofs, and walls and windows that keep heat in during the winter and out during the summer. Some experts also expect self-driving buses and cars to be common, but this is much less certain: the technology has improved quickly, but there are still many problems to solve.
>
> Of course, forecasts about the future are often wrong. In the 1960s, many people were sure that we would all have flying cars by the year 2000. We don't, but we do have phones that can translate languages and maps that know where the traffic is. The most useful prediction may be this one: the cities of 2050 will be designed by people who are at school today.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `do / does / did + V` trong câu khẳng định là nhấn mạnh: thật sự, đúng là. Thường dùng để sửa một suy nghĩ sai.
- `will have + V3` (by 2050) = tới mốc đó việc đã xong. `should have + V3` = lẽ ra nên làm, nhưng đã không làm.
- Bài dự đoán: chú ý các từ chỉ mức chắc chắn (`likely`, `probably`, `may`, `much less certain`). Câu hỏi hay hỏi dự đoán nào kém chắc nhất.
- Câu nối hai bài kiểu so sánh: tìm định nghĩa ở bài này rồi tìm ví dụ khớp ở bài kia.
