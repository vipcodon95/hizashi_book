## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 23) — Tối thứ Sáu xem phim nhà Brooks

> **Friday Movie Night at the Brookses'**
>
> Every Friday, the Brooks family watches a movie together, and Harriet usually comes over with popcorn. Choosing the movie is often harder than watching it.
>
> **Nathan:** OK, I've found a great science fiction movie. It's about a spaceship that gets lost near Jupiter.
>
> **Amy:** Is it scary? I don't like scary ones.
>
> **Nathan:** Only a little. There's one sequence with a monster, but you can close your eyes.
>
> **Amy:** No way. Can we watch the animated one about the singing fox again?
>
> **Helena:** We've seen it four times, sweetheart. Even you were bored last time.
>
> **Harriet:** How about this documentary? It's about the moon landing in 1969, when I was fifteen.
>
> **Amy:** Grandma, you saw it?
>
> **Harriet:** On TV, of course. We didn't have a TV, so we went to our neighbor's house, where about twenty people were sitting on the floor.
>
> **Amy:** Was the TV in color?
>
> **Harriet:** No, it was black and white, and the picture was so unclear that we could hardly see anything. Nobody cared. When the astronaut stepped onto the moon, the room fell silent, and then everyone burst into laughter and applause. I remember it so vividly. I can even remember the song that was playing on the radio that whole summer.
>
> **Helena:** That sounds worthwhile. And it's not scary, Amy, I promise.
>
> **Nathan:** Fine. A real trip to the moon is better than a movie one anyway.
>
> Ninety minutes later, the documentary was over. Amy hadn't closed her eyes once, and she had asked about a hundred questions.
>
> **Amy:** I want to be an astronaut. Or a film-maker. Or both!
>
> **Nathan:** Next Friday, it's my turn to choose. And we're watching the movie about Jupiter.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 24) — Âm nhạc và não bộ

> **Why Music Moves Us: What Happens in Your Brain**
>
> Think of a song that you loved when you were a teenager. You can probably still sing most of the words, and you may even recall where you were when you first heard it. That's why scientists are so interested in music: few other activities use so many parts of the brain at the same time.
>
> When you listen to music, the part of the brain that deals with sound works hard, of course. But so do the parts that control movement, emotion and memory. That is the reason why it is so hard to keep your feet still at a concert, and why a sad song can make you cry even when nothing sad has happened to you.
>
> In 2011, researchers in Canada found that when people heard music they loved, their brains released dopamine, a chemical connected to pleasure. It is the same chemical that the brain releases when we eat good food. Interestingly, the brain started releasing it a few seconds before people's favorite moment in a song, as if it knew what was coming.
>
> Music also seems to protect memories. Many people with serious memory problems in old age, who may no longer recognize their own family, can still sing songs from their youth. Music therapists use this to help them feel calm and connected, and singing is also used to help some people speak again after brain injuries.
>
> But be careful with big promises. In the 1990s, a famous study made many parents believe that playing classical music to babies would make them smarter. Later studies found that the effect was small and lasted only about fifteen minutes, so a baby who listens to an orchestra every day will not become much cleverer because of it. Learning to play an instrument is different: it takes years of practice, and many researchers believe that it can train attention and listening skills.
>
> So if you've always wanted to take up the guitar or join a choir, there's no reason why you should wait. Your brain will thank you.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `, where …` / `, when …` (có dấu phẩy) nói thêm về nơi chốn, thời gian ngay trước nó. Câu hỏi "where refers to…" chỉ cần nhìn danh từ trước dấu phẩy.
- `That's why…` / `the reason why…` = đó là lý do. Câu hỏi "Why…?" thường có đáp án ở câu ĐỨNG TRƯỚC cụm này.
- Trong hội thoại, `Fine.` hay `OK, then.` thường là đồng ý miễn cưỡng. Hãy xem câu sau đó người nói còn đòi gì.
- Bài khoa học hay có đoạn `But be careful…`: câu hỏi thái độ thường hỏi đúng chỗ này.
