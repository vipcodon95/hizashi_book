>

>
> Good morning! This month, each of you will interview an older person about their childhood, so I'll go first. Trevor, please don't hide behind your book; I know you've heard these stories.
>
> I grew up in a small town east of San Diego, in a household of five: Mom, Dad, Grandma, my brother, and me. Grandma taught me the alphabet at the kitchen table before I started preschool.
>
> My brother and I used to get into mischief all the time. We used to chase the neighbors' chickens between our house and theirs and giggle until Mom called us inside. Every summer, a circus came to town, and among all the kids on our road, only I was afraid of the clowns. I also used to be afraid of the darkness, so Grandma left a small light on every night.

>
> Since then, my hometown has changed completely: the farms are gone, and the number of houses has grown a lot. Still, I cherish those years: they made me the person I am today.
>
> This weekend, choose an older person to interview, and on Monday each of you needs to give me a name. And Trevor, you can't choose me!

## Bài đọc 2 — Vì sao ta không nhớ được hồi còn bé xíu?

> **Why Can't We Remember Being Babies?**
>
> Most adults can't remember anything from before the age of three or four. Scientists call this "childhood amnesia."
>
> Strangely, babies are good at remembering: a newborn can already recognize its mother's voice, probably because it heard that voice for months before birth. Keeping those memories is the hard part, as a study at Emory University showed.
>
> Researchers recorded mothers as they talked with their three-year-olds about events such as a trip to the zoo. Years later, the children were asked about the same events. At five to seven, they still remembered more than sixty percent of them, but at eight or nine, less than forty percent. The memories did not disappear in one day; they slowly faded away.
>
> One reason is that the hippocampus, the part of the brain that helps us store memories, is still developing in these early years. Another is that young children don't have many words yet, and memories are easier to keep when we can tell them as stories. Also, a child may need a clear sense of self, an idea of "me," before memories about "my life" can last.
>
> Be careful with your own "first memory," though. In a 2018 survey of more than six thousand people, almost forty percent described a first memory from the age of two or younger. The researchers believe most of these memories never really happened. They were probably built later from family photos and stories told again and again.
>
> So enjoy your parents' stories about your first birthday, but don't expect to find that day in your own memory.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **used to + động từ nguyên mẫu** = thói quen hoặc trạng thái ngày xưa, nay không còn (`I also used to be afraid of the deep darkness`). Câu hỏi về "hồi nhỏ từng…" thường lấy đáp án từ các câu có used to.
- **between** dùng cho hai bên (`between our house and theirs`), **among** dùng cho cả một nhóm (`among all the kids on our road`). Đại từ sở hữu **theirs / mine / ours** thay cho "của ai + danh từ vừa nói": hãy tìm người được nhắc ngay trước.
- **Hoà hợp chủ ngữ – động từ:** `each of you needs`, `one of my favorite memories is`, `the number of houses has grown` đều đi với động từ số ít. Nhận ra chủ ngữ thật (each, one, the number) giúp hiểu câu dài đúng hơn.
- Bài khoa học có nhiều con số (ba bốn tuổi, sáu mươi phần trăm, bốn mươi phần trăm, năm 2018). Ghi bên cạnh mỗi con số là nói về ai, vì phương án nhiễu hay tráo số giữa các ý.
- Câu suy luận (cảm xúc từ hành động, thay đổi "hồi đó – bây giờ", áp dụng vào tình huống mới, nối hai bài): bài không nói thẳng. Ghép hai câu trong bài lại, hoặc đặt tình huống mới cạnh quy luật bài đã nêu.
