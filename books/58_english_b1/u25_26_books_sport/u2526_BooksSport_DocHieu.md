## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 25) — Email câu lạc bộ sách của bà Harriet

> **Subject: Book Club news, and something different for September**
>
> Dear book lovers,
>
> Thank you to the fourteen of you who showed up on Tuesday, even in that heat! My little café has never been so full of opinions. As you know, we read a biography of a young woman who sailed alone around the world, and I've rarely seen this group so divided. Lydia found it informative and full of insight, while Nathan said that it was the most uninteresting book he had ever read, and that he had only looked through the last fifty pages.
>
> We also had a lively debate about paper books and e-books. Lydia, who teaches English, prefers paper, because she likes to write notes in the margins and says that she remembers more that way. Ethan, who reads on his phone during quiet nights at the fire station, said that e-books are lighter, cheaper and easier on the eyes, because you can make the letters bigger. My own eyesight isn't what it used to be, so I'm afraid I agree with Ethan! In the end, we decided that the best book is the one you actually finish.
>
> For September, I'd like to try something different: a book of poetry. I know that some of you are not very enthusiastic about poems, but please give it a chance. Each of us will choose one poem and read it aloud. Nobody has to explain it, and you can choose something short and funny.
>
> Also, our young readers' corner is growing: Tina and Amy have started their own club on Saturday mornings, where they retell fairy tales to the younger kids. Last week, Amy told the story of a wizard in such a scary voice that two children hid under the table.
>
> I'm really looking forward to seeing you all on Tuesday, September 1, at 7 p.m. As always, the coffee is free and the cake is not!
>
> Warmly, Harriet

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 26) — Chấn thương do tập quá sức ở vận động viên trẻ

> **Too Much of a Good Thing: Overuse Injuries in Young Athletes**
>
> Written by: Sunset Bay Hospital Sports Clinic
>
> In March, Trevor Carter got picked for his high school soccer team. By July, he was playing for two teams, training six days a week and running on the weekends to stay fit. Then, in the middle of a summer tournament, he felt a sharp pain in his heel and had to pull out of the final. He hadn't fallen or twisted anything, so at first he thought that it was a minor problem. But when he was still limping a week later, his mother brought him to our clinic.
>
> Trevor had an overuse injury, which happens when the same part of the body is used again and again without enough time to recover. These injuries are becoming more and more common among young athletes, especially those who play only one sport all year. Because teenagers' bones are still growing, the areas near the ends of the bones are softer than in adults, and they can get hurt more easily.
>
> The good news is that most overuse injuries can be prevented. Doctors who work with young athletes recommend at least one or two days off from organized sports every week, and a few months off from the same sport during the year. One simple rule that some experts use: young athletes should not train more hours per week than their age in years. A fifteen-year-old, for example, should do no more than fifteen hours a week. Playing different sports in different seasons also helps, because it uses different muscles.
>
> Pain is not something to play through. If you feel pain that gets worse during exercise, or that is still there the next morning, stop and tell your coach and your parents. And after any hit to the head, the rule is simple: when in doubt, sit it out.
>
> Trevor rested for three weeks and did special exercises for his leg muscles. He is now back on the field, but with a new schedule: one team, five days a week, and Sundays for doing nothing at all. "It was hard to watch the final from the bench," he says. "But I'd rather miss one game than a whole season."

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `A …, while B …` nối hai ý kiến trái ngược. Câu hỏi so sánh hai người hay lấy bằng chứng ở đây.
- Phân biệt `read` (đọc) với `look through` (xem lướt) và `glance at` (liếc qua). Chúng cho biết người đó đọc kỹ tới đâu.
- `get + V3` thường mang nghĩa bị động: got picked = được chọn, got hurt = bị thương.
- Câu áp dụng quy tắc: viết quy tắc ra (giờ tập ≤ số tuổi), thay số trong câu hỏi vào, rồi so.
