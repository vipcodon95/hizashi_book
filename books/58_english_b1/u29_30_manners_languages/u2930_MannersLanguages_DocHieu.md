## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 29) — Email của Tracy về bạn cùng phòng mới

> **Subject: My new roommate (and a Thanksgiving question)**
>
> Hi Leah,
>
> Classes started three weeks ago, and I finally have a roommate: Camila, a transfer student who grew up in a small town in southern Brazil. She's studying engineering like me, she laughs at all my bad jokes, and she cooks much better than I do. She started learning English at fourteen, and she speaks it fluently, with an accent that I love.
>
> Living together has been a lesson in manners, though, for both of us. On her first day, she hugged me, my friend Jenna and even the man who delivered our pizza. Later she told me that in her family, everyone hugs everyone, and that a handshake feels cold to her. I'm a handshake person, so the pizza man and I had a very awkward moment. But she has had surprises too. She was shocked that I eat lunch at my desk while I'm working. At home, her mom made everyone sit down together, and nobody was allowed to leave the table early. When I told her that my parents had us put our phones in a basket at dinner, she said, "See? Your family isn't so different from mine."
>
> I've learned not to cut in when she's telling a story, because she always finishes with something funny. Of course, we've realized that neither of us speaks for a whole country. Her cousins in the city do things very differently, and you and I don't even agree on how to eat pizza!
>
> Now my question: can I bring Camila home for Thanksgiving? Her family is too far away, and the flight is too expensive. She's a little nervous. "What if your family thinks I'm too loud? What if I use the wrong fork?" she keeps asking. I told her that Dad will make her eat three plates of food and that Biscuit will love her more than anyone. Do you think Mom will say yes?
>
> Love, Tracy

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 30) — Học ngoại ngữ có bao giờ là quá muộn?

> **Is It Ever Too Late to Learn a Language?**
>
> Many people believe that only children can learn a new language well, and that adults who start later will always struggle. The truth is more interesting.
>
> Young children do have one clear advantage: accent. Children who start a second language early typically learn to pronounce it like native speakers, while people who start as adults commonly keep some accent from their mother tongue, even when they become fluent. Grammar is a different story. A large study in 2018, which tested almost 670,000 people online, found that the ability to learn grammar stays strong until about the age of seventeen. However, to reach the level of a native speaker, it seems that most people need to start before about the age of ten.
>
> Adults also have advantages of their own. They usually learn faster at first, because they can read, take notes, memorize lists and understand rules. They also know what they want to say: a child may not need words like "rent" or "insurance", but an adult newcomer may need them in the first week.
>
> What about children who grow up with two languages at home? Some parents worry that this will cause confusion or make their children start talking later. Research does not support this: bilingual children usually learn to talk at about the same age as other children. They sometimes mix the two languages in one sentence, but that is usually not a mistake: it shows that they know both languages and can choose the best word.
>
> So, for adults, what works best? Experts suggest getting used to hearing the language every day, even for a short time, and speaking from the start, even if you make mistakes. Being misunderstood is uncomfortable, but it is part of the process. And don't overthink your accent: people care much more about whether they can understand you than about whether you sound like a native speaker.
>
> It may never be too late. But it's always a good time to start.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `make + người + V` = bắt làm; `let + người + V` = cho phép; `have + người + V` = bảo, nhờ làm. Câu hỏi nghĩa của từ hay hỏi đúng ba động từ này.
- `What if…?` trong lời nhân vật thường là nỗi lo. Câu hỏi cảm xúc sẽ có đáp án dạng "worried about…".
- Khi nói về văn hoá, người viết cẩn thận thường khoanh vùng: `in her family`, `in my town`. Đừng chọn phương án gán thói quen cho cả một đất nước.
- Bài nghiên cứu: phân biệt các con số (`seventeen` cho ngữ pháp, `ten` cho trình độ bản ngữ) và việc mỗi con số nói về điều gì.
