## Phần 2 — Blog của Ethan về cách gọi 911

> **What I Wish Everyone Knew About Calling 911**
>
> Written by: Ethan Hayes, firefighter
>
> I've been a firefighter in San Diego for six years, and I've answered thousands of emergency calls. Most callers do a great job, but every week I see small mistakes that cost us time, and in an emergency, every minute counts.
>
> Mistake number one: starting with the story instead of the location. When people are frightened, they often explain what happened before they say where they are. But if the call is cut off, we can't help you, because we don't know where to go. So say the address first, then explain the problem. If you don't know the address, look for street signs or a building nearby.
>
> Mistake number two: hanging up too early.
>
> Stay on the line until the operator tells you that you can hang up. They may need to give you instructions, for example how to stop someone's bleeding while you wait for us.
>
> Mistake number three: calling 911 for things that are not emergencies. A neighbor's loud party or a lost cat is not an emergency, and calls like these can keep the line busy for someone who is having a heart attack. Use your city's non-emergency number instead. It's a good idea to save it in your phone today.
>
> Finally, here's something that many people don't know: in many areas, including San Diego, you can send a text to 911 if you can't speak safely. But a voice call is still faster, so call if you can, and text only if you can't.
>
> I hope you never need to call us. But if you do, remember: where first, then what, and don't hang up.

## Phần 3 — Bài khoa học về giấc ngủ của tuổi teen

> **Why Teenagers Can't Fall Asleep Early**
>
> Many parents believe that teenagers stay up late because they are lazy or spend too much time on their phones. Phones are part of the story, but scientists say that the main reason is inside the body.
>
> Everyone has a body clock that tells us when to feel sleepy and when to wake up. It is controlled partly by a chemical called melatonin, which the body produces when it gets dark. During the teenage years, this clock moves later: many teenagers do not start to feel sleepy until about 11 p.m., up to two hours later than when they were younger.
>
> At the same time, teenagers still need a lot of sleep: experts recommend eight to ten hours a night. If a student falls asleep at 11:30 and has to get up at 6:30 for school, they get only seven hours, and after five school days, the missing hours add up. Not getting enough sleep can make it harder to pay attention, remember new information and control your mood, and it is also dangerous for young drivers.
>
> So where do phones fit in? The bright light from screens can tell the brain that it is still day, so the body produces melatonin later than it should. Messages and videos also keep the mind active, which makes falling asleep even harder.
>
> Scientists suggest a few simple habits. Put your phone away about an hour before bed, ideally in another room. Try to go to bed and get up at similar times every day, even on weekends, because sleeping until noon on Sunday makes Monday morning feel even worse. And get some sunlight in the morning: it helps your body clock know when the day has started.
>
> Some schools in the United States have moved their start times later so that students can get more sleep, and studies show that students there are less sleepy in class and arrive late less often.

## Phần 4 — Bài kiến thức về cầu Cổng Vàng

> **The Bridge That Fog Couldn't Hide**
>
> For many visitors, the Golden Gate Bridge is the picture of San Francisco: two tall orange towers rising out of the fog above the entrance to the bay. But less than a hundred years ago, many people believed that a bridge in that place could never be built.
>
> Before the bridge, people who wanted to travel north from the city had to take a ferry across the water. The strait is about a mile wide, the water is deep, the currents are strong, and the wind and fog arrive suddenly from the ocean. Some experts said that the wind would blow the towers down, while others worried that ships would not be able to see the bridge in the fog.
>
> Work finally began in January 1933, and the bridge was opened in May 1937, after about four years of construction. At that time, its main span, the part between the two towers, was the longest of any bridge in the world, and it kept that record until 1964. During construction, a safety net was hung under the bridge, which was unusual at the time, and it saved the lives of nineteen workers who fell.
>
> The famous color was almost an accident. The steel arrived covered in a red paint that was only meant to protect it, and the architect liked it so much that he decided to keep a similar color. He felt that the warm orange fit well with the hills and the sky, and it also made the bridge much easier to see in the fog.
>
> You may have heard that painters start again at one end as soon as they reach the other. That is not quite true. A team of painters does work on the bridge all year, but they only paint the parts that need it most, because wind, fog and the salt in the sea air are always damaging the paint. Nearly ninety years after it opened, the bridge that "could never be built" is still carrying thousands of cars every day.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 10 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 14 phút, Phần 3 và Phần 4 khoảng 18 phút mỗi phần.
- **Đọc câu hỏi trước:** gạch chân từ khoá (số, tên, ngày) rồi dò bài. Thông báo và blog có tiêu đề nhỏ như `Date:` hay `Mistake number two:` giúp tìm nhanh.
- **Câu NOT / TRUE:** kiểm từng phương án với bài. Phương án có chữ trong bài nhưng sai chi tiết vẫn là phương án sai.
