## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 32) — Lịch sử ngắn của vắc-xin

> **From Cows to Cells: A Short History of Vaccines**
>
> In the 1700s, smallpox was one of the most feared diseases in the world. It killed millions of people, and many of those who survived were left with deep marks on their skin. But some people had noticed something strange: milkmaids, the women who milked cows, rarely seemed to catch it.
>
> An English doctor called Edward Jenner was curious. Many milkmaids had caught cowpox, a mild disease from cows. There must be a connection, he thought. In 1796, he conducted an experiment that would never be allowed today. He took material from a cowpox spot on a milkmaid's hand and put it into a small cut on the arm of an eight-year-old boy, James Phipps. Weeks later, he gave the boy smallpox material. The boy did not get sick. Jenner's theory was proved, and the word "vaccine" comes from vacca, the Latin word for "cow".
>
> Jenner was not the first to protect people in this way. In parts of Asia and Africa, people had used a more dangerous method for hundreds of years: they gave healthy people a small amount of material from a smallpox patient. Jenner's method was much less dangerous, and it spread quickly.
>
> About ninety years later, the French scientist Louis Pasteur made a vaccine against rabies, a disease that animal bites can pass to people. Since then, researchers have developed vaccines against many diseases, including measles, polio and the flu. Most of them work in a similar way: they show the body a weak, dead or harmless part of a germ, so that the body learns to recognize it and is ready to fight the real thing.
>
> The biggest success came in 1980, when the World Health Organization announced that smallpox had been completely wiped out. It is still the only human disease that has ever been removed from the whole planet. Today, scientists in laboratories are working on new types of vaccines, and some of them are likely to change medicine in ways that Jenner never imagined.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `was supposed to + V …, but …` = lẽ ra phải làm nhưng không làm. Câu hỏi nghĩa thường có phương án "should have…, but didn't".
- `police` luôn đi với động từ số nhiều (police are), còn `news` thì không đếm được (the news spread). Đừng nhầm news là số nhiều.
- `There must be…` là suy đoán chắc chắn, chưa phải điều đã chứng minh. Câu hỏi hay đặt bẫy "he had already proved it".
- Câu suy luận "What can we guess…?": tìm câu nói rõ điều CHƯA biết (`the police are still looking for the thief`).
