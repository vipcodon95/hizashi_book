## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 21) — Blog của Chloe về mạng xã hội

> **What Six Months of Freelancing Taught Me About Social Media**
>
> Posted by: Chloe Bennett
>
> When I left my job at Harriet's café in December, I had three clients and a lot of hope. Today I have eleven clients, and almost all of them found me online. I've been posting my work on social media every day since January, and I've learned a few things that nobody told me at the start.
>
> First, people want to see the process, not just the final design. My most popular post wasn't a finished logo; it was a short clip of me drawing twenty bad ideas before I found the good one. Hundreds of people left comments, and one of them, a bakery owner in Oregon, became my biggest client.
>
> Second, engaging with people matters more than the number of followers. I answer every comment, and I meet up with local clients face-to-face whenever I can.
>
> Online messages are quick, but they are often very short. A typical chat with a client looks like this:
>
> **Marco:** Free Thursday?
>
> **Chloe:** Can't. Friday?
>
> **Marco:** Sounds good. Usual place?
>
> **Chloe:** See you there!
>
> Third, I've become much more careful about privacy. I never post a client's work until they say it's OK, and I don't share where I live or which café I'm working in today. Last month, someone created a fake account with my name and photos and asked my followers for money. Luckily, I warned everyone right away, and as far as I know, nobody fell for it.
>
> Finally, social media is a tool, not a life. I've been trying to stay off my phone after 9 p.m., and I sleep much better now. My friend Leah, who works at a hospital, says that's no surprise at all.

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 22) — Bản tin khu phố: cứu chó và tin đồn trên mạng

> **Dog Rescued from Storm Drain; Online Story Proved False**
>
> Maple Street Neighborhood News · June 12
>
> Trapped in a storm drain for almost nine hours, a young dog named Pepper was rescued by San Diego firefighters on Tuesday evening. Pepper had run away from her owner during a walk in Pine Street Park and had climbed into the drain, which is only about two feet wide. Two children heard her crying at around 5 p.m. and told a parent, who called the fire department.
>
> Built in the 1960s, the drain is too narrow for an adult to enter, so the team used a small camera to find Pepper and then slowly guided her toward an opening with food. "She was scared and tired, but she wasn't hurt," said firefighter Ethan Hayes, who was part of the team. "The real heroes are the two kids who heard her and didn't give up."
>
> But by the next morning, a very different story was spreading online. A post that was shared more than 3,000 times claimed that the firefighters had charged the owner $500 for the rescue. It also said, supposedly from a witness, that someone had thrown the dog into the drain on purpose. Neither claim was true. Asked about the post, the fire department said officially that it never charges for rescues, and the owner explained that Pepper had simply run off. The "witness" turned out to be an account that someone had created the day before.
>
> How can you avoid falling for stories like this? Check if a reliable news source is reporting the same thing. Look for proof, such as names, dates and photos that you can check, and be careful with posts that make you very angry, because angry people share more and check less. Before you share, ask yourself one question: do I know that this is accurate, or do I just want it to be true?

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- `have been + V-ing + since/for`: việc bắt đầu trong quá khứ và vẫn đang tiếp tục. Đừng chọn phương án nói việc đã dừng.
- Câu lược bớt trong hội thoại (`Can't.`, `Sounds good.`, `Usual place?`): tự điền phần bị bỏ dựa vào câu ngay trước.
- Mệnh đề phân từ đầu câu (`Built in the 1960s, the drain…`) luôn nói về chủ ngữ ngay sau dấu phẩy.
- Từ như `supposedly`, `claimed`, hay dấu ngoặc kép quanh một chữ ("witness") là tín hiệu người viết không tin điều đó.
