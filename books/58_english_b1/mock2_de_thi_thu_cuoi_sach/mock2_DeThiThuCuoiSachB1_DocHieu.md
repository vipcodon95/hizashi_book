# B1 Mock Test 2 (Final) (Đề thi thử B1 (cuối sách))

> **Luyện cho:** Đề thi thử cuối sách · ôn toàn bộ khoá B1, phiếu `b1.001` – `b1.160` (40 unit).
>
> **Nhân vật:** Harriet Brooks, Nathan Brooks, Leah Carter · tháng 1, năm 6
>
> **Mục tiêu:** Đề thi thử 40 câu, làm trong 60 phút (theo format đọc VSTEP bậc 3: 4 bài, 280–400 từ mỗi bài). Đề có 4 phần × 10 câu, khó dần: thông báo lớp học ở thư viện → blog của Leah về những tháng đầu làm bác sĩ → bài khoa học về bạch tuộc → bài nêu quan điểm về tuần làm việc bốn ngày. Đề kiểm tra ý chính và mục đích, thông tin cụ thể, câu NOT / TRUE, nghĩa của từ trong ngữ cảnh, thái độ và quan điểm của người viết, áp dụng thông tin vào tình huống, và khoảng 13 câu suy luận. Bài đọc dùng ngữ pháp của cả khoá B1: be to do, should have done, wish, so that, mệnh đề quan hệ, bị động với is believed to, although / despite.

## Phần 2 — Blog của Leah về những tháng đầu làm bác sĩ

> **What My First Months as a Doctor Taught Me**
>
> Written by: Dr. Leah Carter, resident doctor
>
> When I graduated from medical school, I thought that the hardest part was over. I was wrong. In medical school, you study diseases. In the hospital, you meet people, and people are much more complicated than books.
>
> The first lesson was about time. During my first night shift, I had twelve patients and only one pair of hands. I wanted to do everything perfectly, so I did everything slowly. An experienced nurse took me aside and said, "Doctor, decide what can't wait, and do that first." I have used that advice every day since then.
>
> The second lesson was that nurses know a lot. They spend more time with patients than doctors do, so they often notice small changes first. I grew up with a nurse in the family, so I should have known this already, but I had to learn it again in the hospital.
>
> The third lesson was the hardest: admitting mistakes. In my second month, I wrote the wrong amount on a patient's medicine order. A pharmacist noticed it before the patient took anything, and nobody was hurt. I felt terribly embarrassed, and part of me wanted to say nothing. Instead, I told my supervisor. She thanked me and explained that hospitals are safer when people report mistakes, because that is how everyone learns to prevent them.
>
> I am still tired most of the time, and I still drink too much coffee. But when a patient who arrived frightened goes home smiling, I remember why I chose this job, and I wouldn't change it for anything.

## Phần 3 — Bài khoa học về bạch tuộc

> **The Octopus: An Alien in the Ocean?**
>
> If scientists ever wanted to design a creature that seemed to come from another planet, they could hardly do better than the octopus.
>
> To begin with, an octopus has three hearts. Two of them pump blood through its gills, where it takes oxygen from the water, and the third pumps blood around the rest of its body. Its blood is blue, not red, because it uses copper instead of iron to carry oxygen.
>
> Its brain is just as unusual. An octopus has around 500 million nerve cells, which is similar to a dog, but about two-thirds of them are in its arms, not in its head. This means that each arm can touch, taste and even make simple decisions on its own.
>
> Octopuses are also famous for their intelligence. In Indonesia, researchers have filmed octopuses carrying coconut shells across the sea floor and later using them as a shelter. In aquariums, some have learned to open jars, and a few have escaped from their tanks. Some even seem to recognize individual people: workers at one aquarium reported that an octopus often shot water at the same worker, but never at anyone else. One famous octopus in New Zealand is believed to have climbed out of its tank at night, crossed the floor and slipped down a pipe that led to the ocean.
>
> Perhaps most amazing of all, an octopus can change the color and even the texture of its skin in less than a second, so that it looks exactly like a rock or a piece of coral. Strangely, scientists believe that most octopuses cannot see colors in the way that we do, and they are still trying to understand how the animals match their surroundings so well.
>
> Yet for all their intelligence, most octopuses live surprisingly short lives, often only one or two years. They are not aliens, of course. But they are a reminder that the most extraordinary creatures on Earth may be living right under the waves.

## Phần 4 — Bài nêu quan điểm về tuần làm việc bốn ngày

> **The Four-Day Week: Work Less, Do More?**
>
> For most of the last hundred years, a normal working week has meant five days and around forty hours. Recently, however, a growing number of companies have been testing a different idea: four days of work, with the same pay.
>
> The biggest trial so far took place in the United Kingdom in 2022, when around sixty companies and nearly three thousand workers took part for six months. At the end, the great majority of the companies decided to continue with the shorter week. Most said that the amount of work done had stayed about the same, while workers reported less stress, better sleep and fewer sick days.
>
> Supporters say that the results are not surprising. When people know they have only four days, they waste less time in long meetings and focus on what really matters. A rested worker, they argue, is a better worker, and an extra day off gives people time for their families, their health and their hobbies. Some companies also found that it became easier to hire new workers, because many people saw the shorter week as a great benefit.
>
> Not everyone is convinced. Critics point out that the companies in the trial chose to take part, so they were probably the ones most likely to succeed. A four-day week may also be much harder for hospitals, schools and shops, which need people there every day. In some jobs, workers simply have to fit five days of work into four longer days, which can make them more tired, not less.
>
> So, is the four-day week the future? In my view, the honest answer is "for some, but not for all". The trials suggest that many office jobs could work this way without losing much, but a nurse or a bus driver cannot simply stop working on Fridays. Perhaps the most useful lesson of these experiments is not about the number of days, but about how much time we waste when we are at work.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 10 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 14 phút, Phần 3 khoảng 17 phút, Phần 4 khoảng 19 phút.
- **Thông báo:** để ý các con số và điều kiện (`three days`, `24 hours`, `ages 8 – 12`). Câu hỏi hay đảo hai con số cho nhau.
- **Blog kể chuyện:** bài thường chia theo `The first lesson… The second lesson…`. Dùng các mốc này để tìm nhanh đoạn cần đọc.
- **Bài khoa học:** câu chủ đề đầu đoạn (`Octopuses are also famous for their intelligence.`) cho biết ví dụ phía sau minh hoạ cho điều gì.
- **Bài nêu quan điểm:** tách ba phần: ý kiến ủng hộ (`Supporters say`), ý kiến phản đối (`Critics point out`), và ý kiến người viết (`In my view`). Câu hỏi "writer's opinion" chỉ lấy ở phần thứ ba.
