## Bài đọc 1 (Unit 3) — Mitchell: khi kho lưu trữ không khớp lời kể của hàng xóm

> **When the Archive Disagrees with My Neighbor**
>
> Mitchell Carter, volunteer interviewer for the Pine Street Library oral history project "Voices of Maple Street"
>
> My interview with Harriet Brooks was to have taken an hour. It took three afternoons, four pots of coffee and, by the end, a small disagreement between my neighbor and the city's newspaper archive that I have been turning over in my mind ever since.
>
> Harriet remembers arriving on Maple Street in 1978, a young teacher with two suitcases, in the same week as the great storm, when the old fire station on Pine Street flooded and the firefighters rowed a boat down the middle of the street. She told the story vividly, with the confidence of someone who has told it many times. The archive tells a different story. The flood happened, and there is even a photograph of the boat, but it happened in 1980, two years after she moved in.
>
> My first reaction, I am ashamed to say, was the historian's small thrill of catching a witness in an error. My second was to wonder why the error had taken that particular shape. It would have been easy to chalk the mistake up to the passing of time, correct the date quietly and move on.
>
> Psychologists have studied such errors for decades. On the morning after the space shuttle Challenger exploded in January 1986, the psychologists Ulric Neisser and Nicole Harsch asked a group of university students to write down exactly how they had heard the news. More than two years later, they asked the same students again. Many of the new accounts differed markedly from the originals, yet the students were highly confident, and their confidence bore no relation to their accuracy. Some, when shown the pages in their own handwriting, preferred the later memory.
>
> In hindsight, Harriet's error looks less like carelessness than like meaning. For her, both events stood for the same thing: the moment when a stranger became a neighbor. Had it not been for the flood, she told me, she might never have met half the street, because everyone spent that week carrying sandbags together. Over time, memory had quietly moved the flood closer to her arrival, where it belonged emotionally if not chronologically. The archival record is not wrong, and neither, in its own way, is Harriet. The task is not to choose between them but to reconcile them, because memories are ephemeral in their details and stubborn in their meaning, whereas dates are exactly the opposite.
>
> So which version should the archive keep for posterity? Both, I think, and side by side. The newspaper gives us the date; Harriet gives us what the date meant. An oral history that has been corrected until it agrees with the documents is no longer an oral history; an archive without memories is merely a list of things that happened to nobody in particular.
>
> When I showed Harriet the photograph of the boat, she studied it for a long time. "Well," she said at last, "I would have liked to have been there. I suppose, in a way, I was."

## Bài đọc 2 (Unit 4) — Truyện: nhà tài trợ muốn đặt tên mình cho công viên

> **The Name on the Gate**
>
> The letter from Grayson Pell arrived on a Tuesday, and by Thursday everyone on the Pine Street Pocket Park committee had read it at least three times. Mr. Pell, a property developer whose name was on half the new buildings downtown, was offering $250,000 for a playground and a water fountain. In return, he asked for three things: that the park be renamed Pell Park, that skateboards be banned, and that the gates close at six.
>
> **Walt:** Let's be honest, we can't afford to say no. Do you know how many tasting nights it would take to raise a quarter of a million dollars?
>
> **Priya:** Of course I do. I'm the treasurer. But I keep coming back to one question: who decides what happens in this park? Two hundred and fourteen neighbors answered our survey before we built it. Mr. Pell is one person.
>
> **Walt:** A generous person.
>
> **Priya:** Generosity with conditions isn't a gift. It's a contract.
>
> **Walt:** That's a bit stringent, isn't it? He isn't trying to muscle in on anything. He just wants to be remembered.
>
> Tina, who had been invited as the committee's youth representative and had so far said nothing, raised her hand.
>
> **Tina:** The skateboarders are the only teenagers who actually use the park after school. If you ban them, it'll be a park for little kids and their parents, and the rest of us will be back in the parking lot of the supermarket.
>
> Helena, who had held the committee together for three years mainly by letting everyone finish their sentences, waited until the room was quiet.
>
> **Helena:** I don't think Mr. Pell is a villain, and I don't think we should treat him like one. I'd like to write back, thank him sincerely, and offer him a different arrangement: we accept the money for the playground, with a plaque that names his family, but the name of the park and its rules stay with the neighbors.
>
> **Walt:** And if he says no?
>
> **Helena:** Then we'll have a smaller playground that belongs to us.
>
> Mr. Pell did say no. His reply was short and, Walt thought, rather hurt. He called the committee "parochial" and wrote that he did not give money "in order to be thanked on a piece of metal". Helena read the word "parochial" twice and decided, after some thought, that it was not entirely unfair. They had been a little insular. So the committee did something it had never done before: it asked the whole neighborhood. Walt, who had been sure that the committee would succumb in the end, admitted that he had underestimated how much the neighbors cared about one small piece of land.
>
> Eleven months later, the playground opened. It was smaller than the one in Mr. Pell's letter, and it had been paid for by more than six hundred people, from a retired judge to a group of skateboarders who sold lemonade every Saturday in June. ## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- Bài luận có **hai phản ứng** của người viết (`My first reaction… My second…`). Câu hỏi thái độ hỏi vì sao người viết bác bỏ phản ứng đầu.
- Cấu trúc `X if not Y` (`emotionally if not chronologically`) = đúng theo X, dù không đúng theo Y.
- Truyện C2 ít khi có kẻ xấu rõ ràng. Câu hỏi về nhân vật (`Helena`, `Mr. Pell`) thường xoay quanh **cách họ cư xử**, không phải ai đúng ai sai.
- Chi tiết cuối truyện (tấm biển trên cổng) thường **trả lời lại** xung đột chính. Đối chiếu với điều kiện ban đầu (tên, giờ đóng cửa).
