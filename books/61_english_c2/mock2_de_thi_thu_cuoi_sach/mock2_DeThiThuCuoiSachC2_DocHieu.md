## Phần 1 — Thông báo: The Neighbors' Kitchen tròn một năm

> **The Neighbors' Kitchen Turns One**
>
> Anniversary Tasting Fair. Saturday, October 19, 11 a.m. to 3 p.m., in the kitchen and on the sidewalk outside Harriet's, Maple Street
>
> A year ago, we opened a shared kitchen in the old bakery with two ovens, one rule and no idea whether anyone would come. Since then, the kitchen has been booked for an average of more than five hours a day, well above the four hours we needed to cover our costs. Thirty-one cooks now use it regularly, and three of them have since opened businesses of their own: a food truck, a market stall and, to our great pride, a small bakery two blocks away. Over the year, cooks from at least fourteen different family traditions have used the ovens, with dishes ranging from tamales and dumplings to a cake that one family insists is older than the street itself.
>
> How the fair works. Tasting tokens cost $2 each and can be exchanged for a small plate at any table. Children under 12 receive two free tokens. All money from token sales goes directly to the cooks, except for one dollar in every ten, which will fund a free cooking course for teenagers next spring.
>
> Allergies. Every dish will carry a card listing its main ingredients and any common allergens, such as nuts, wheat, milk or eggs. If you have a serious allergy, please speak to the cook before eating; the cards are a guide, not a guarantee, since all the dishes are prepared in the same kitchen.
>
> Bring your own container. Visitors who bring a reusable plate or box will receive a third free token. Last year's opening produced eleven bags of paper plates; this year, we would like to produce fewer than three.
>
> Share a recipe. Harriet will be collecting family recipes for a small neighborhood cookbook. Recipes may be written in any language; volunteers will help with translation, and each recipe will appear with the name of the family it came from, unless you would prefer otherwise. The fair will go ahead whatever the weather. In case of rain, the tables will simply move inside the café and the kitchen, which will be crowded but, we suspect, rather cheerful.
>
> Harriet will, of course, be baking scones. She has asked us to point out that they are not available in exchange for tokens. "Some things," she says, "are still my business."

## Phần 2 — Bài khoa học: vì sao ngáp lại lây

> **Why Are Yawns Contagious?**
>
> There is a fair chance that you will yawn before you finish this article. Simply reading about yawning, or seeing a picture of someone doing it, is enough to trigger a yawn in many people. Scientists have known about this effect for a long time; explaining it has proved far harder. Even thinking about yawning can be enough, which is something you may be discovering at this very moment.
>
> For many years, the most popular explanation was empathy. If we catch yawns because we unconsciously share other people's states, the theory went, then more empathetic people should catch them more easily, and we should catch them more readily from friends and family than from strangers. Some early studies seemed to support this, and the idea became something close to accepted wisdom.
>
> The picture became more complicated in 2008, when researchers in London reported that 21 out of 29 dogs yawned after watching a human yawn. It was a delightful result, and it was widely reported as evidence that dogs share our feelings. Later attempts to repeat the experiment, however, produced mixed results: some found a similar effect, while others found a much weaker one or none at all, and the researchers themselves had noted that the dogs might simply have been mildly stressed.
>
> The most careful test of the empathy theory so far came in 2014, from a team at Duke University. They showed a three-minute video of people yawning to 328 healthy volunteers and counted how often each one yawned. About two-thirds yawned at least once, but individuals varied enormously, and each person's tendency stayed remarkably stable across repeated sessions. When the researchers looked for an explanation, empathy was not meaningfully linked to the results. The only factor that mattered independently was age, with older people yawning less, and even age explained only about eight percent of the differences.
>
> In other words, most of the variation remains unexplained. This does not prove that empathy plays no role; it suggests that, if it does, the role is much smaller than the popular story claims. Science, it seems, is still catching up with something that every tired student in a warm classroom has always known. So if you have yawned while reading this, do not take it as proof of your kind heart. Take it, rather, as a reminder that some of the most familiar things we do are still, scientifically speaking, a mystery.

## Phần 4 — Thư bà Harriet gửi "you", người hàng xóm đã ở Maple Street tám năm

> **A Letter to the Neighbor Who Learned Our Language**
>
> Dear neighbor, Eight years ago, at the end of a hot August, a card appeared under your door inviting you to visit my café. You came, you ordered a coffee in very careful English, and you apologized three times for a mistake that I did not notice. I have been meaning to write to you ever since, and the library has finally given me an excuse.
>
> They have asked me to describe, for their collection, someone who changed the street. I could have chosen many people, but I chose you, partly because you never seemed to realize how much you were noticing. You found our dog when he ran away. You learned the bus routes before any of us had bothered to. You sat in my café on Saturday mornings swapping lessons with a teenager who is now an engineer, and you asked questions about our habits that made us realize we had never thought about them at all. You also told me, in your second year, that my soup needed more salt, which nobody on this street had dared to say in all the years I have been making it.
>
> People sometimes say that learning a language means learning to think like the people who speak it. I am not so sure. Nobody on this street thinks alike; we cannot even agree about the best way to make soup. What I have watched you learn is something subtler and more valuable: when a sentence means more than it says, when "fine" does not mean fine, and when the kindest thing to say is nothing at all.
>
> You still make mistakes, of course. So do I, in the only language I have ever spoken. Last spring I told a whole archive the wrong year for a flood, with complete confidence. Fluency, I have come to think, is not the absence of mistakes but the ability to keep talking after you make one, and to laugh, and to let someone else laugh with you.
>
> So here is my request. The library would like to record your story, too: where you came from, what surprised you here, and what you still find strange. Please say yes. This street has been listening to itself for a long time; it is high time it heard a voice that learned to speak to it from the outside.
>
> With love and, as always, a scone waiting for you on Wednesday, Harriet

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 12 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 3 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 4 khoảng 18 phút.
- **Thông báo:** câu áp dụng có thể cần **cộng hai điều kiện** (dưới 12 tuổi + mang hộp riêng). Đọc hết các mục trước khi chọn.
- **Bài khoa học C2:** theo dõi số phận của một giả thuyết: phổ biến → bị thách thức → kiểm tra kỹ → kết luận thận trọng (`This does not prove…; it suggests…`).
- **Truyện:** nhiều nhân vật nói cùng lúc. Câu hỏi hàm ý thường hỏi một chi tiết nhỏ (từ chối cà phê, cái điện thoại) nói lên tính cách hay cảm xúc.
- **Thư / bài luận cá nhân:** người viết dùng ví dụ của chính mình (lỗi về trận lụt) để củng cố lời khuyên. Câu hỏi "why does she mention…" hỏi chức năng của ví dụ đó.
