## Phần 1 — Thông báo mở phòng lưu trữ "Voices of Maple Street"

> **Voices of Maple Street: The Listening Room Opens**
>
> Pine Street Library, second floor. Opening day: Saturday, May 18, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
>
> Over the past year, volunteers have recorded more than 120 conversations with people who have lived or worked in our neighborhood, some of them for over half a century. Their memories, ranging from the great flood of 1980 to the first neighborhood parade, are now available to anyone who wishes to listen. Some speakers describe events that the newspapers never mentioned; others remember famous events in ways that no newspaper could.
>
> Listening booths. Six quiet booths with headphones are available during library hours. Booths may be reserved for up to one hour at a time; at busy times, we ask visitors to limit themselves to thirty minutes so that others may have a turn.
>
> Online access. Recordings will be published online only once the speaker, or, where the speaker has since died, their family, has given written permission. Some recordings may therefore be available in the library long before they can be heard at home.
>
> Share your own story. Have you lived, worked or grown up near Maple Street? Book a free forty-five-minute recording session with a trained volunteer. Speakers under 16 must be accompanied by a parent or guardian. You will receive a copy of your recording, and you may ask for it to be withdrawn at any time, without giving a reason.
>
> A note on accuracy. These are memories, not documents. Where a speaker's account differs from the written record, we have left the recording as it is and added a short note giving the documented facts. We believe that both have something to teach us. If you notice a mistake in a recording about your own family, please tell us. We would rather add a second voice than remove the first.
>
> Volunteers wanted. We are looking for people to transcribe recordings and to interview speakers who would rather tell their story in Spanish, Vietnamese or Tagalog. No experience is needed, only patience and a good pair of ears. To book a booth or a session, ask at the front desk or call 619-555-0176.

## Phần 2 — Bài khoa học: vì sao không tự cù mình được

> **Why You Cannot Tickle Yourself**
>
> Try it now: run your fingers lightly along the sole of your own foot. Unless you are very unusual, very little will happen. Yet the same touch from another person can leave you helpless with laughter. This small paradox has turned out to reveal something profound about how the brain works.
>
> The most widely accepted explanation comes from research by the neuroscientist Sarah-Jayne Blakemore and her colleagues at University College London. Whenever you move, they argued, your brain sends a copy of the movement command to a region at the back of the head called the cerebellum. The cerebellum uses this copy to predict exactly what the movement will feel like. When the real sensation arrives, it matches the prediction, and the brain turns down its response.
>
> A touch from someone else cannot be predicted in the same way, so it arrives as a surprise and is felt at full strength. The researchers tested this idea in an ingenious way. Volunteers moved a handle that controlled a robot, which in turn stroked the palm of their other hand with a piece of soft foam. When the robot copied the movement immediately, the touch felt dull. But when a short delay was added, or when the direction of the touch was slightly changed, volunteers rated it as more ticklish. The prediction no longer matched the sensation, and so the surprise returned.
>
> Why would the brain bother to cancel out sensations that it causes itself? The most cogent answer is that it would otherwise be overwhelmed. Every step, every breath and every movement of your clothes produces sensations. A brain that paid equal attention to all of them would hardly notice the one sensation that matters, such as an insect landing on your arm. Filtering out the predictable leaves attention free for the unexpected.
>
> Some researchers have gone further and posited that the same system helps the brain tell the difference between what we do and what is done to us. Some people who hear voices that others cannot hear seem, in certain studies, less able to cancel out their own touch, a finding which hints at a link, although it is far from proven.
>
> The lesson is a strange one. Your inability to tickle yourself is not a failure of the brain but a sign of how well it is working.

## Phần 4 — Bài luận của cô Lydia: ca ngợi sự buồn chán

> **In Praise of Boredom**
>
> Lydia Park, English teacher, Westview High School
>
> In 2014, a team of psychologists led by Timothy Wilson published a study with a finding that sounds like a joke. They asked volunteers to sit alone in an empty room, with no phone and nothing to read, and simply think for between six and fifteen minutes. Most found it unpleasant. In one version, participants could give themselves a mild electric shock if they wanted to, a shock that most had earlier said they would pay to avoid. Two-thirds of the men and a quarter of the women pressed the button at least once.
>
> It is tempting to treat this as proof that we cannot bear our own company. I would be more cautious. Critics have pointed out that the shock was brief, that some participants were probably just curious, and that most people still spent almost all of their time thinking. Suffice it to say that the study shows how uncomfortable boredom can feel, not that it is unbearable.
>
> Discomfort, however, is not the same as harm. Boredom is a signal, much like hunger: it tells us that our attention needs something it is not getting. It is not a sign that something is wrong with us; it is a sign that the world around us has stopped asking anything of our minds. For most of human history, the only answers available were to look around, to make something, or to let the mind wander. Today we have a fourth answer in our pockets, and it is so effective that the signal hardly has time to sound.
>
> I noticed this during my school's phone-free experiment two years ago. On the first day, students complained of boredom with real anguish. By the third, some of them had started doodling, arguing, inventing games and, in one case I will never forget, writing a rather good poem about the classroom clock. None of them would have done so had the boredom been switched off at the first sign.
>
> I am not arguing that boredom is pleasant, or that we should seek it out. I am arguing that we should stop treating it as an emergency. The space between the signal and our response is where imagination tends to live, and it seems a pity to fill every inch of it.

## 💡 Mẹo làm bài

- **Quản lý thời gian:** 60 phút cho 4 bài. Phần 1 khoảng 12 phút, Phần 2 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 3 khoảng 15 phút, Phần 4 khoảng 18 phút.
- **Thông báo:** câu áp dụng luôn có một điều kiện (`at busy times`, `under 16`, `only once … permission`). So con số trong câu hỏi với con số trong bài.
- **Bài khoa học:** tách **lời giải thích chính** (tiểu não dự đoán) với **ý mở rộng chưa chắc chắn** (`posited`, `hints at`, `far from proven`).
- **Truyện:** lời nhân vật thường nói một đằng nghĩa một nẻo (`he would think about it`, `Just like it`). Câu hỏi hàm ý hỏi nghĩa thật.
- **Bài luận:** cấu trúc `I am not arguing that X. I am arguing that Y.` → Y là quan điểm. Phương án chứa X là bẫy.
